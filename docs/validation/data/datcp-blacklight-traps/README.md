# DATCP field-crop moth traps: black light network and western bean cutworm, black cutworm and corn earworm pheromone networks, 2020–2026

The Wisconsin Department of Agriculture, Trade and Consumer Protection (DATCP) runs a network of black light traps (13 sites in 2020, falling to 6 in 2025–2026) read weekly from late April or May to September, which counts ten moth species, and a network of western bean cutworm pheromone traps (36–83 sites a year) read weekly from mid-June to August. Its black cutworm pheromone network (30–102 sites a year, read weekly from late March or April to early June) is tabulated too, for 2021–2023 and 2026. We use them to check the timing of the insect degree-day models of part H, batch H2 (`docs/models-plan.md`, `tmp/part-h-plan.md`): European corn borer, western bean cutworm, black cutworm, variegated cutworm and corn earworm. `scripts/check_insect_traps.py` reads these files.

## Files

- `weekly.csv`: the black light network, one row a site, week and species: `year`, `site` (DATCP's name), `county`, `week_end` (the reporting week's last day, ISO date), `species`, `count` as published (a number; `-` for 2020's "――", no report; empty for a blank cell; `NA` as 2026 prints it: see Caveats for what each means by year), `note` and `source` (the file it was read from). Species: black cutworm, celery looper, corn earworm, dingy cutworm, European corn borer, forage looper, spotted cutworm, true armyworm (DATCP's "armyworm", "TA" or "Taw"), variegated cutworm, western bean cutworm. DATCP doesn't split European corn borer by pheromone race, and stalk borer isn't counted.
- `wbc-pheromone-weekly.csv`: the western bean cutworm pheromone network, one row a site and week: `year`, `site`, `county`, `week_end`, `count` as published (a number, empty for a blank, `NA` in 2026, a number with `*`, `**` or `***` in 2024 for a count over two or three weeks), `note`, `source`.
- `bcw-pheromone-weekly.csv`: the black cutworm pheromone network, one row a site and week: `year`, `site`, `county`, `week_end`, `count` as published (a number; empty for a blank cell; `NA` as 2026 prints it), `note`, `source`. Added 2026-11-21 for the black cutworm model's default biofix (`scripts/check_cutworms.py`).
- `bcw-pheromone-sites.csv`: each black cutworm pheromone site (`site`, `county`, `years`) with an approximate `lat`, `lon` and how it was located (`located`): the row of `sites.csv` with the same county and name where there is one, else the Census Bureau's 2023 gazetteer (the place, or the town in the site's county) for the name without its direction suffix ("Lancaster N" → Lancaster; "Mt." read as Mount, "Argyl" as Argyle, "Leeds/Doylestown" as the town of Leeds), else the county's centre (5 sites: Albertville, Rolling Ground, Cedar Lake, Hub City, North Bend W).
- `cew-pheromone-weekly.csv`: the corn earworm pheromone network, 2021–2026 (added for the corn earworm model's default biofix, part H), one row a site and week, with the same columns as the western bean cutworm file: `count` as published (a number; empty for a blank, which is no report or the trap not yet set or already down; `NA` in 2026, no report; `-` once in 2023, no report).
- `sites.csv`: each site and network, the years it appears, an approximate location (`lat`, `lon`, rounded to 0.01°) and how it was located (`located`): DATCP's 2026 trap-network survey layer where the site (by county and name) is in it, else the Census Bureau's 2023 gazetteer (the place, or the town in the site's county) for the name without its direction or number suffix ("Portage N" → Portage), else the county's centre (7 pheromone sites: Atlas, Bryant, Curtis, Fall City Prairie, Rusk, Sumner, West Bloomfield). Cooperators' names are left out. Sites are as DATCP names them each year: the same name in two years may be two trap positions, and in the black light network "Wausau" and "Wausau North" (the 2022–2025 year-end summaries) are the traps called "Wausau E" and "Wausau N" in 2022's weekly files and in 2026.

## Caveats

- What a blank means changes by year. Black light: in 2020 "――" is no report; in 2021–2023 zeros are printed, so a blank is no report; in 2024–2025 zeros are left blank, so a blank is a zero or no report (indistinguishable; `check_insect_traps.py` reads it as zero); in 2026 the PDF prints `NA` for every zero, while the web page prints 0 and keeps NA for no report. The survey layer shows three 2026 site-weeks with no reading (Arlington June 3, Wausau E May 6 and July 15), noted in `weekly.csv`; elsewhere 2026's NA is read as zero. Pheromone: a blank is no report (or the trap not yet set), and 2026's PDF says NA means no report.
- A count covers the week ending on its date (in 2020 Thursday to Wednesday), so a first catch or a percentile is known only to within the 7 days before it. 2020's week ending July 8 covers July 1–8, and June 25–30, 2020 wasn't published.
- 2024 pheromone counts marked `*` cover two weeks and `**` or `***` three (the PDF's footnote); they are kept as printed, under the week they're printed in.
- Black light catches of most of these species are small: European corn borer 59–293 moths a year over the whole network, corn earworm 39–108, variegated cutworm 11–72, black cutworm 28–86. A site's flight percentiles rest on a handful of moths; the pheromone network (thousands of moths a year) is the stronger record for western bean cutworm.
- Arlington's black light trap caught "European corn borer" in mid-May in 2021–2023 (7 in the week ending May 19, 2021; 7 on May 12, 2022; 14 on May 11, 2023), well before DATCP's own expectation of the first moths at 374 degree days (base 50 °F), around late May to early June in southern Wisconsin (the 2020 bulletins: first moths "by May 30 and June 5" in 2018 and 2019). Whether these are early moths or misidentified ones isn't known; they pull early the first-flight dates of those years.
- Sites change: the network shrank from 13 sites (2020) to 6 (2025–2026), and its sites are in the southern and central counties (Wausau and Marshfield are the northernmost after 2021). Hancock appears only in 2020, Antigo last in 2021, Janesville, Pardeeville and Sparta last in 2022 (Janesville and Sparta in 2022's weekly files only, with one week reported), Manitowoc and Prairie du Chien last in 2023 (Manitowoc with one week reported), and Arlington is missing from 2025.
- Locations are approximate: the 2026 survey layer gives where the trap stood in 2026, which may not be where it stood before; gazetteer points are town or village centres, and pheromone traps sit in fields nearby.
- The 2025 pheromone summary spells Trempealeau County "Trempeauleau"; it's kept as published.
- DATCP offers each year's data in Excel on request (tracy.schilder@wisconsin.gov), which would settle the blank cells of 2024–2025.
