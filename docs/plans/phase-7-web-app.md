## A. Architecture decisions (settled 2026-11-26, D117–D123)

- [x] **A1. Where pages get their data.** **Settled → D117** (Ben, 2026-11-26). Recommended: Inertia props for the page and its content (a model's metadata and Markdown, a topic page, the methods, the changelog, the user and their settings, the shared `status` and `today`); the browser fetches `/api/v2` for data (weather, model runs, risk, tiles, climate). D57 made the frontend the API's first client, so a number on a page is the API's number; the map and charts change with the reader's settings without a page visit; ETags and the API's caching come for free. One typed client (`lib/api.ts`) over `@/types/api`: the envelope, `meta.warnings` shown in the API's words, errors, `Retry-After`, an abort on a superseded request. Alternative: props for everything (WISP 3's way), which means a page visit per setting change and a second path to every number.
- [x] **A2. URL state.** **Settled → D118** (Ben, 2026-11-26), not as recommended: the address bar stays on the plain path and a Share button gives the link with the settings (provisional). Recommended was: the view lives in the query string (frontend.md: `?layer=…&time=…&at=…&z=…`), through one helper that maps a typed state object to and from the query, writing with `history.replaceState` as settings change (no Inertia visit, so the map isn't torn down) and `pushState` only for a change a reader would go Back from (another layer, another site). Shared links reproduce the view; defaults are left out of the URL.
- [x] **A3. The map.** **Settled → D119** (Ben, 2026-11-26). Recommended: one `DataMap` on MapLibre from WISP 3's core, with our data tiles as a `raster-dem` source (`encoding: "custom"`, the TileJSON's factors) colored by a `color-relief` layer from `/palettes`, so a palette or scale change recolors on the GPU with no request; the value under the pointer decoded from the same tile's pixels (fetched once, kept); OpenFreeMap's light style with its labels above the data; WISP 3's overlays; Save image from late-blight-db. Zoom past 8 enlarges zoom 8 (the API's tiles stop there). Risk: `color-relief` is new in MapLibre and untried here; a custom WebGL layer is the fallback (M more).
- [x] **A4. Saved sites, groups and preferences in the browser, then synced.** **Settled → D120** (Ben, 2026-11-26). Recommended: one versioned store in `localStorage` (`lib/storage`), shaped as the Django models will be: sites keyed by coordinates (F1), groups, and preferences at three levels (all sites, group, site) holding shown crops and models, map view and chart defaults, with groups' choices joined by union (F3, F11, F12). Signed in, the server is the source and the browser a cache; at first sign-in the browser's sites merge into the account by coordinates, and nothing is lost. Up to 50 sites (F4).
- [x] **A5. How WISP 3's base is ported.** **Settled → D121** (Ben, 2026-11-26). Recommended: copied into the same paths under `frontend/lib/` (so later fixes in either app carry across by diff), with WISP's route helpers replaced by a small `lib/routes.ts` generated by `bin/types` from Django's URL names (the props types already come from there), and its tests ported with each file. A note at the top of each ported file names its WISP 3 source.
- [x] **A6. Accounts.** **Settled → D122** (Ben, 2026-11-26). Recommended: django-allauth with login by emailed code and link (allauth's "login by code"), no passwords (architecture.md §8), our own Svelte pages over allauth's headless API rather than its Django templates, sessions as now (CSRF as now). Settings: units, beta models, service updates, alerts. Allauth's admin covers user management.
- [x] **A7. Email.** **Settled → D123** (Ben, 2026-11-26). Recommended: Django templates (HTML and text) in WISP 3's layout, sent by Procrastinate jobs; one daily digest per user at a chosen hour and event alerts (a model reaching a level, a forecast frost) at most once per event; unsubscribe by signed link. Ben: `agweather@cals.wisc.edu` through Amazon SES, as WISP 3 (TODO.md); mail on a queue and worker of its own.

## B. The work, in tracks (sizes as the phases; each page lands with its axe and smoke tests, light and dark)

1. **Foundation** (L): WISP 3's base (above) ported; dependencies to WISP's versions; the header (Map · Sites · Models · Tools · Climate · Data, place search, theme, settings), the footer, the settings dialog with the units switch (F10); `lib/api.ts` (A1), the URL-state helper (A2), `lib/routes.ts` (A5); the glossary's first terms; vitest helpers and Playwright's `scan()` with outside services stubbed; the home page (F2: search a place, what's in season, saved sites, links by task, data through when).
2. **The map explorer** `/map` (XL, P0): `DataMap` and its data-tile layer (A3), the layer picker by group and crop (`/layers`, `/models`), time (a day, an hour, a range with `stat`, season to date, the scrubber with −7, −1, +1, +7, Today and play), `roll=` smoothing, degree-day settings and biofix, climate layers (`climate=`), palette and scale in the browser, the title chip, legend, credit and newest hour, click a cell (outline, value, the panel with recent days, forecast and a chart, a link to the site), overlays, Share, Save image, GeoTIFF/NetCDF of the view, TileJSON. Then: insect layers by time to act (M, D36; Phase 6's events), the cropland overlay (D116: serving the 30 m CDL ourselves or CropScape's service, for Ben), the hillshade beneath (Phase 6 D6's leftover), and the next 7 forecast days in `ops.precompute.DAYS` once the scrubber steps through them.
3. **Sites** (L, P0): saved sites and groups (A4: add by click, search or coordinates; name, reorder, delete; CSV import and export), `/sites` (cards lazy-loading `/risk`), the site page `/sites/{lat},{lng}` (header; Overview with today, forecast and risk now; Weather; Models; Degree days; Data), a model at a site (inputs, output and risk stacked on one time axis through observed, forecast and projection, stage bands, action windows, "since a date", the NEWA-style results table), and the two feeds (`/sites/models/{id}` and one site across models).
4. **Models and topics** (L, P0): the catalog by crop, the model page (generated sections, guidance by risk level, region and season, inputs, version and changelog, citations, the training area, fitted curves on two clocks), the explorer (`POST /models/{id}/evaluate`), topic pages from `content/topics` with footnote citations, the methods page from `/methods`.
5. **Tools** (L): the degree-day calculator with crop presets, oak wilt, freeze dates and odds, the day-of-year calendar (P0); the crop GDD outlook (S once 3 lands), the alfalfa growth and season planner (S–M; F5's pure function in `agmodels` and a small endpoint), potato volunteer (P1).
6. **Climate** (M, P1): normals, this year against normal, frost odds, monthly counts against normal (D88), departure maps through the map explorer.
7. **Data and reference** (M): downloads (point and grid builders, yearly files), data status (`/status`, the stale-data banner), the changelog and API-use pages designed (D112, with the banner and nav link), about, glossary, methods, accessibility statement, contact; legacy redirects (D62's map of v1 and VDIFN URLs).
8. **Accounts and email** (L): A6 and A7; synced sites, groups and preferences; settings; the digest and alerts (built on Phase 6's events and `/risk`), unsubscribe; the BCC announcement to current v1 subscribers.
9. **Carried in** (from Phase 6's review): the days still missing after the gap fill, flagged for every model (S–M): `RunResult.missing` as `<input>_missing` and a `meta.warnings` line on `/models/run`, and a per-cell count kept with the stored seasons and model maps so a map warns; pages say "includes N estimated days" and "N days missing" (D45). The ten-year projection measured with the clock in June for one model and a dashboard's 5–8, the < 1 s target held to those. The model page content's structured guidance (models.md part I's write-ups) continues beside the pages.

Order: 1 first (everything sits on it); then 2 and 3, the two P0 centers, side by side; 4 and 7 beside them; 5, 6 and 8 after. Each track's pages are planned in detail as it starts (layout and components named, API calls listed, the questions for Ben), as Phase 6's parts were.
