## Enhancement: satellite land fluxes (ALIVE) — to explore, not planned

Logged 2026-11-22 (Ben: a UW colleague pointed to it). **ALIVE** (Advanced Baseline Imager Live Imaging of Vegetated Ecosystems; Stoy, Desai, Ranjbar et al., UW–Madison BSE/AOS/SSEC, NSF/NOAA/WARF funded) estimates land–atmosphere fluxes from GOES-R ABI with gradient-boosting models trained on AmeriFlux/NEON eddy covariance towers. The product Ben was sent is **ALIVE_Hourly** on Earthmover's Arraylake marketplace (https://app.earthmover.io/marketplace/6a2b3bb933da0de9e826697c): GPP, ecosystem respiration (RE), latent heat (LE) and sensible heat (H), with model and data uncertainties, from a histogram gradient boosting regressor trained on eddy covariance, GOES-R and HRRR. The listing is a JavaScript page we couldn't read; its grid, extent, coverage, latency, license and version are unknown. Related: the GPP framework (Remote Sensing of Environment 2025, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0034425725001634: CONUS, 5-minute, latency under a day, R² 0.67–0.70 half-hourly, 0.78 daily, 111 towers), ALIVE_ET (HESS 30, 4927–4955, 2026, doi:10.5194/hess-30-4927-2026: daytime ET R² 0.74 half-hourly, croplands 0.66 half-hourly and 0.84 daily; night R² 0.24; worse under cloud and in winter; no HRRR yet, listed as future work), and public Arraylake repos `wisc-alive/ALIVE_v1-1` (GPP 2022–2023) and `ALIVE_v1-2024` ("updated daily"). Project site https://alive-abi.github.io/alive/.

Update 2026-11-22: Paul Stoy (lead author) says the paper describing the hourly product was submitted today. Revisit once it's out (its validation over croplands and at night, the grid, latency and license).

**Earthmover itself** (where ALIVE is hosted; read 2026-11-22): Icechunk is an open-source (Apache 2.0) transactional storage engine for Zarr (commits, branches, consistent reads during writes) that runs as a library on S3 or local disk with no service; Arraylake (catalog, permissions, credential vending over your own bucket), Compute (a managed Rust engine serving stored arrays as tiles, EDR, WMS, SQL) and the Marketplace are hosted SaaS, free up to 10 GB, 1 private repo and 50 compute credits a month, otherwise priced by sales; no academic plan listed, no self-hosting found. It doesn't shrink our server: our load is ingest, rollups and models computed on request, which Compute doesn't run. Pieces worth remembering: Icechunk as the format if older years move from EBS to S3 (project-plan's "older years can move to S3 later"), or to give readers consistent snapshots during writes; reading ALIVE (or other marketplace data) needs only a free account.

- **Why it could matter:** it's an observed estimate of *actual* crop water use (LE → ET) and growth (GPP), which nothing in v2 has: our ET0 (D8) is a reference for a well-watered grass, not what a field used. Candidates: an actual-ET layer beside ET0 (and for WISP, a check on its ET0 × crop coefficient), a drought or crop-stress signal (actual against reference ET), GPP as a green-up/season index. None feeds an existing model; it would be new layers, not a weather input.
- **Not production ready, as far as we can tell (2026-11-22):** a research product with no stated license, version policy, latency or maintenance commitment; the hourly product has no paper or validation we could find; the published skill on croplands is moderate (R² 0.66 half-hourly ET) and weakest at night, under cloud and in winter/snow; ABI's fixed-grid projection (~2 km) would need regridding to NDFD, as HRRR does; the GOES-16 → GOES-19 handover (2025) is a seam to check; it depends on one lab's funding.
- **To explore when there's a reason:** read the listing in a browser (grid, extent, start, latency, license, auth, size); ask the authors about license, maintenance and the hourly product's paper; compare its LE/ET with our ET0 and with Wisconet's or Arlington's towers over a season; check how it handles Wisconsin's cloudy springs. If it holds up, it's a post-launch enhancement (M: an adapter reading Zarr from Arraylake, a regrid, one or two map layers), never a launch dependency.

## Enhancement: soil from Wisconet's station models — to explore, not planned

(Ben, 2026-11-25.) Soil temperature and moisture (D52–D55, D110, D111) are to be revisited as a whole, so nothing about them is fixed yet (layers or depths included). A member of the Wisconet team is training random forest models on Wisconet's sensors (5, 10, 20 and 50 cm; one model per variable and depth), predicting statewide on a 1 km grid from gridMET weather and antecedent conditions (precipitation, ETr, air temperature, shortwave), POLARIS soil texture, latitude, elevation and slope. Her figures on withheld data (March–October 2026, with stations installed after training): temperature R² 0.95–0.97, RMSE 1.2–1.6 °C; water content R² 0.70–0.86, RMSE 0.059–0.074 m³/m³. Against ours (`docs/validation/weather/soil.md`, `soil-moisture.md`): ERA5-Land's moisture r 0.65–0.73 (R² about 0.45–0.55) and an error of 0.10–0.12, mostly a per-station offset that soil texture would explain; temperature MAE 1.5–1.8 °C over all seasons. When her work is ready, worth asking before porting:

- Withheld by station or by time? A leave-stations-out score is the one that says how it does between stations. And how it does in winter (frozen soil, snow cover), which the withheld months leave out.
- Running it here: on our archive's inputs instead of gridMET (D84 already fits gridMET to the archive), every day within hours (gridMET lags a day or two), and on the forecast's days; the model files' size and license.
- Wisconsin only: the launch box would have her values in Wisconsin and ERA5-Land elsewhere, with a seam at the state line; or her approach used to correct ERA5-Land's level by texture across the box (her suggestion: station data correcting or checking ERA5-Land).
- Report our sources in her terms (R², RMSE, by depth, on the same withheld months and stations) so the two compare directly.
