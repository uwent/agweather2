**Numba's cache in the production image** (found 2026-11-06): the kernels (`agdata.fastdecode`, `agmodels.degree_days_grid`) are `cache=True`, which writes compiled code to `__pycache__` beside the package. In the image that folder may not be writable by the app's user, and then every web and worker process recompiles at start (seconds each, not the ~0.5–0.8 s of loading measured in development). Set `NUMBA_CACHE_DIR` to a writable volume in `infra/` (shared by `web` and `worker`), or compile the kernels when the image is built (a build step that imports and warms them as the app user), and check a container's start time and first custom DD map after deploy. S.

**Past years on S3** (Ben, 2026-11-22; to settle with the deployment configuration, M–L): plan to hold most past years in S3 rather than on the EBS volume, unless the performance hit makes it untenable, and keep the current year (and whatever the fast paths read most) on EBS. Measure first: §3's latency cases (`manage.py latency`) and a season map, a ten-year projection at a point and a climatology read with the past years in S3, since every chunk read goes from a local-disk millisecond to tens of milliseconds over the network (the past-years copy and `timecopy` are the reads that matter). Format: Icechunk (open source, Apache 2.0, a library over S3 with no service; see "Enhancement: satellite land fluxes (ALIVE)" for Earthmover) is the candidate to weigh against plain Zarr on S3: it adds consistent snapshots and commits, which could also stand in for parts of the write lock. Earthmover's hosted pieces (Arraylake, Compute) don't reduce the server, since our load is ingest, rollups and models on request.

**The archive's January trim** (D3, D87; M; needed by January 1, 2028, when 2016 goes): nothing removes the archive's oldest year yet. A year lives in the hourly and daily stores, the cumulative store, the past-years copy (`pastyears`), the season-read copy (`timecopy`), the models' ingredients and stored maps (`modelmaps`), the ERA5-Land store (`era5store`), the yearly NetCDF downloads and the dataset status. An `archive trim` command (`ReportingCommand`, `--check`, under the write lock) and a January job remove them together, the daily store last, since `series.archive_years` (and so maps' refusal, D87, and points' `in_archive`) read the daily store's presence: a half-removed year must still read as outside the archive. After it: the January `update_climatology` run derives gridMET's version of the dropped year (its files are kept as an overlap year; `ops.climatology.plan`), refits on the shorter overlap and rebuilds `last30`, `last10` and `last5`. Check what reads a fixed first year (`gridmet.FIRST_YEAR`, `api.rolled.read_start`, tests that assume 2016) and the projection's years. Test on a copy of the dev archive by trimming 2016.
