Two aphids commonly settle and multiply on potato in Wisconsin: the green peach aphid (_Myzus persicae_) and the potato aphid (_Macrosiphum euphorbiae_). The buckthorn aphid (_Aphis nasturtii_) does so less often.[^uw-vegento-pvy][^cornell-vectors] Their feeding seldom costs much yield by itself; they matter because they carry potato leafroll virus and potato virus Y (PVY), which makes them most important to seed growers.[^wickwar-2023][^uc-ipm-aphids] UW–Madison calls the green peach aphid the most destructive and most insecticide-resistant aphid in Wisconsin.[^uw-vegento-aphids] Most of the aphids that spread PVY never settle on potato at all; they have a page of their own, [Aphid PVY Vectors](/topics/aphid-pvy-vectors).

## Identification

Aphids are small, soft-bodied, pear-shaped insects with a pair of short tubes (cornicles) at the rear of the abdomen. Nymphs never have wings; adults may or may not.[^uw-vegento-aphids][^wickwar-2023]

- **Green peach aphid:** about 3/32 to 1/8 inch long. Wingless adults are pale to bright green or yellowish green, and nymphs pale green, yellow or pinkish. Winged adults have a black head and thorax and a large dark patch on the back of the abdomen. The cornicles are of medium length, slightly swollen, with dark tips. Colonies usually start on the lowest leaves.[^wickwar-2023][^uc-ipm-aphids][^cornell-vectors][^uw-vegento-aphids]
- **Potato aphid:** larger, about 9/64 inch, green or pink, with red eyes, long legs and long, slender cornicles often the color of the body. It moves quickly when disturbed. Colonies often start on young, growing tips and spread through the whole plant.[^wickwar-2023][^uc-ipm-aphids][^cornell-vectors][^umass-potato-aphid][^uw-vegento-aphids]
- **Telling them apart:** with a hand lens, look at the bumps (tubercles) between the bases of the antennae. On the green peach aphid they lean in toward each other; on the potato aphid they angle away.[^wickwar-2023][^uc-ipm-aphids]
- **Buckthorn aphid:** small; wingless forms are yellow to greenish, with dark tips on the cornicles.[^cornell-vectors]

A winged aphid in a potato field may belong to one of many species just passing through, and a lone winged aphid is hard to identify when you don't know what plant it came from. Small groups of wingless aphids, often with white cast skins, mean a colonizing species is settling in.[^wickwar-2023][^uw-vegento-pvy]

## Life cycle

Both main species switch hosts with the seasons. Green peach aphids spend the winter as eggs on peach, cherry, plum and related trees; potato aphids on wild and cultivated roses; buckthorn aphids on buckthorn.[^wickwar-2023][^uw-vegento-aphids][^cornell-vectors] In spring, females hatch and bear live young without mating, and winged females fly to summer hosts, potato among many others. There, overlapping generations of wingless females build colonies, and crowding brings on winged forms that spread to new plants. In late summer and fall, winged aphids return to the winter hosts to mate and lay eggs.[^wickwar-2023][^uw-vegento-aphids]

UW–Madison counts both species' overwintering in Wisconsin among the reasons PVY has become a bigger problem in the state.[^uw-vegento-pvy] Many green peach aphids also arrive on southerly winds. Their main flight comes in late summer, and in central Wisconsin's fields they are rarely found on leaves before mid-August; potato aphids are rare there until late July and build through August.[^agweather-green-peach-aphid][^agweather-potato-aphid]

## Damage

**Feeding.** Aphids suck sap from leaves and stems. Large colonies can weaken plants, and potato aphids feeding on growing tips can deform leaves and cause dieback.[^wickwar-2023][^uc-ipm-aphids][^umass-potato-aphid] The sugary honeydew they excrete grows sooty mold.[^uw-vegento-aphids]

**Potato leafroll virus.** An aphid picks up leafroll by feeding on an infected plant and can then spread it for the rest of its life. Only aphids that colonize potato spread it, and the green peach aphid is by far the most effective.[^hamm-2026][^wickwar-2023][^uc-ipm-aphids] Plants grown from infected seed are stunted, with rolled, leathery lower leaves, and lose the most yield. Infection during the season rolls and yellows new leaves and can cause net necrosis, brown streaks inside the tubers of some varieties such as Russet Burbank, which worsens in storage.[^hamm-2026][^wickwar-2023][^uc-ipm-aphids]

**Potato virus Y.** The colonizing aphids spread PVY efficiently, the green peach aphid perhaps best of all, and their colonies carry it from plant to plant within a field. [Aphid PVY Vectors](/topics/aphid-pvy-vectors) covers the virus, seed certification and the other aphids that spread it.[^cornell-vectors][^uw-vegento-pvy]

## Monitoring

- Scout weekly. Aphids cluster in hot spots, often along southern and western field edges, tree lines and drive rows, where migrants land. Note whether the aphids you find have wings.[^uw-vegento-pvy]
- **Green peach aphid:** take one whole leaf from the lower half of the canopy on each of 25 plants, at 10 or more places in each field. UW–Madison's thresholds are more than 10 aphids per 100 leaves in seed fields and more than 30 in table stock or processing potatoes.[^uw-vegento-aphids]
- **Potato aphid:** take leaves from the tips of 35 plants at 10 or more places in each field. The thresholds are more than 20 aphids per 100 leaves in seed potatoes and more than 50 in table stock or processing potatoes.[^uw-vegento-aphids]
- Scouts in central Wisconsin also count potato aphids in sweep-net samples.[^agweather-potato-aphid]
- The [Aphid Suction Trap Network](https://suctiontrapnetwork.org/) catches flying aphids each week at sites across the Midwest, several of them in Wisconsin. Traps show aphids moving through the region, not the numbers in your field.[^suction-traps][^uw-vegento-pvy][^wickwar-2023]

## Management

**Natural enemies.** Lady beetles, lacewing larvae, syrphid fly larvae and parasitic wasps all eat or parasitize aphids, and heavy rain and fungal diseases in humid weather also cut their numbers.[^uw-vegento-aphids] In seed potatoes, where a few aphids already call for action, they seldom keep numbers low enough on their own.[^wickwar-2023] Protect them by avoiding broad-spectrum insecticides and by using selective ones against Colorado potato beetle.[^wickwar-2023][^umass-potato-aphid][^mahr-aphids] Pyrethroid sprays can also set aphids moving through the landscape.[^uw-vegento-aphids]

**Cultural controls.** Plant certified seed low in virus. Remove volunteer potatoes and nightshade weeds, which host both the aphids and the viruses, and avoid excess nitrogen.[^wickwar-2023][^uc-ipm-aphids]

**Insecticides:**

- **At planting:** a systemic insecticide keeps aphids down for the first several weeks and protects early-generation seed, but it wears off by midseason.[^wickwar-2023][^uw-vegento-pvy]
- **Foliar sprays:** start when scouting finds small colonies of wingless aphids or counts pass the thresholds. Treat the whole field, then again a week later. Effective groups include the neonicotinoids (IRAC group 4A: imidacloprid, thiamethoxam, acetamiprid, clothianidin, dinotefuran), sulfoxaflor (4C), flupyradifurone (4D), pymetrozine and pyrifluquinazon (9B), afidopyropen (9D), tolfenpyrad (21A), spirotetramat (23), cyantraniliprole (28) and flonicamid (29).[^uw-vegento-pvy][^uw-vegento-aphids][^irac-moa] Ground sprays reach into the canopy better after it closes.[^uw-vegento-pvy]
- **Resistance:** green peach aphids have evolved resistance to several classes of insecticides worldwide;[^bass-2014] UW–Madison names the organophosphates (group 1B) in particular.[^uw-vegento-aphids][^irac-moa] Make no more than two applications in a row from one group, then switch groups if colonies persist.[^uw-vegento-pvy]
- **Oils:** seed growers spray paraffinic oils weekly from about two weeks after full emergence, mainly against PVY from aphids passing through ([Aphid PVY Vectors](/topics/aphid-pvy-vectors)). Oils added to an insecticide program improve aphid control and seem to spare most beneficial insects.[^uw-vegento-pvy][^wickwar-2023]

For products and current registrations in Wisconsin, see [Commercial Vegetable Production in Wisconsin](https://vegpath.plantpath.wisc.edu/resources/a3422/) (A3422); in neighboring states, the [Midwest Vegetable Production Guide](https://mwveguide.org/guide). For home gardens, see [Wisconsin Horticulture's aphid page](https://hort.extension.wisc.edu/articles/aphids-2/).

## AgWeather's models

Two models show typical pressure for the time of year, not a forecast for your field. Each reads either the calendar or degree days (base 39.2 °F, 86 °F cutoff, from January 1).

The [green peach aphid model](/models/green-peach-aphid) shows the typical weekly catch of winged green peach aphids in Wisconsin's suction traps, from curves UW–Madison Vegetable Entomology fitted to catches in 2010 and 2014 to 2023. By default it reads the calendar: the flight builds from about August 8, peaks about September 1 and falls to half its peak by about September 13 (by degree days, the peak comes at about 3,410). Tested on years and traps left out of the fit, the calendar described the catches better than degree days: the flight follows the season and migrants from the south more than local warmth. Neither could time one trap's season, which can be weeks off the typical one.[^agweather-green-peach-aphid]

The [potato aphid model](/models/potato-aphid) shows the typical number of potato aphids per 25 sweeps in commercial potato fields in central Wisconsin, mostly the Central Sands, from 2014 to 2025. By default it reads degree days: counts reach a tenth of their highest at about 2,520 (about August 1) and half at about 3,360 (about August 30), and are still near their highest when scouting ends in mid-September. Degree days described a held-out year's counts better than the calendar in 10 of 12 years; the calendar timed the rise as well or better. The fields were treated at planting and sprayed when counts reached a threshold, so the curve shows pressure in managed fields.[^agweather-potato-aphid]

Use the models to plan scouting: include potato aphids in sweeps from mid-July, and in seed potatoes keep aphid and virus protection in place through late August and consider killing vines before the green peach aphid flight peaks. Your own leaf counts decide when to spray. Outside Wisconsin for the traps, and central Wisconsin for the fields, the models come with a warning; northern seed fields aren't in the potato aphid data.[^agweather-green-peach-aphid][^agweather-potato-aphid]
