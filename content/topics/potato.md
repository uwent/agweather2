The potato (_Solanum tuberosum_) is grown in Wisconsin for the fresh market, for processing and for seed.[^ness-2010][^a3422-2026] Much of a grower's season is timed from the crop's emergence, including the split applications of nitrogen and the first fungicide sprays for early and late blight.[^a3422-2026][^colquhoun-2026] This page describes the crop's stages in Wisconsin and links to the pests and diseases AgWeather models.

## Potatoes in Wisconsin

Wisconsin is the third-largest potato-growing state, after Idaho and Washington. Production is concentrated in the Central Sands, in the Wisconsin River watershed; Portage, Waushara, Langlade and Adams are the leading counties.[^uw-system-potatoes] The region's sandy soils lie over aquifers left by glacial meltwater, which suits irrigated crops, and the spread of irrigation after World War II revived the state's potato industry.[^colquhoun-2026][^ness-2010] Sandy soils can also be planted earlier in spring.[^uw-system-potatoes]

Potatoes are planted from early April through early May on sandy soils, from mid-April through early June in northern Wisconsin, and as late as early July on muck soils. The usual guide is to plant once the soil is 50 to 55 °F, close to the temperature of the seed pieces. Wisconsin law requires growers of more than 5 acres to plant certified seed.[^a3422-2026]

## Growth stages

- **Sprout development:** the seed piece breaks dormancy and sprouts.
- **Vine growth:** after emergence, leaves and stems grow, and underground stems called stolons form.
- **Tuber initiation:** under good conditions, tubers begin to form at the stolon tips 2 to 3 weeks after emergence. This is the stage most sensitive to water stress.
- **Tuber bulking:** tubers grow in size and starch content.
- **Maturation:** vines age and die back, and the tuber skin sets over the 2 to 3 weeks after tubers stop growing.[^colquhoun-2026]

Growers hill the rows at planting and again shortly after emergence, and split nitrogen between emergence and tuber initiation on sandy soils.[^a3422-2026] Vines are killed 14 to 21 days before harvest so the skins set.[^a3422-2026]

## What the stages mean for pests and diseases

- **Emergence:** the season's counts for [early blight](/topics/early-blight) and [late blight](/topics/late-blight) start at about 50 % emergence. Wisconsin's thresholds for the first fungicide spray are 300 potato physiological days (P-days) for early blight and 18 disease severity values (DSVs) for late blight.[^uw-potato-eb][^gevens-2026][^colquhoun-2026]
- **Early season:** overwintering [Colorado potato beetle](/topics/colorado-potato-beetle) adults move to emerging potatoes in spring. Their larvae feed for several weeks in summer, and the beetles can strip the canopy.[^a3422-2026][^colquhoun-2026]
- **Late May to early July:** potato leafhoppers blow in from the south; their feeding burns leaf edges ("hopperburn") and can cut yield by 20 to 30 %.[^a3422-2026][^colquhoun-2026]
- **Aphids:** green peach aphids, efficient carriers of plant viruses, are the third key insect pest (see [aphids that spread potato virus Y](/topics/aphid-pvy-vectors)).[^a3422-2026][^colquhoun-2026]
- **Flowering through bulking:** early blight rises after flowering and spreads fastest while tubers bulk.[^shrestha-2025]
- **After harvest:** tubers left in the field can survive a mild winter and come up the next year as [volunteers](/topics/potato-volunteers), which host Colorado potato beetle and late blight.[^cohen-2024] Cull piles are a source of late blight and must be handled properly.[^colquhoun-2026]

## Monitoring

- Record each field's planting and 50 % emergence dates. The early and late blight models count from emergence.[^uw-potato-eb]
- Read UW–Madison Vegetable Pathology's in-season [Vegetable Crop Updates](https://vegpath.plantpath.wisc.edu/2026/08/23/vegetable-crop-update-aug-23-2026/), which give P-days and DSVs for early, mid and late plantings across Wisconsin's potato areas.[^gevens-2026]
- Scout the whole field, including the edges near tree lines, and learn where on the plant each disease appears.[^colquhoun-2026]

## Management

Many problems are settled before planting: certified seed, rotation away from potatoes and their relatives, and managing culls and volunteers reduce the diseases and insects that start each season.[^a3422-2026][^colquhoun-2026][^uw-potato-eb][^cohen-2024] Irrigation scheduling keeps the crop's water steady, especially at tuber initiation, without keeping leaves wet longer than needed.[^a3422-2026][^colquhoun-2026][^uw-potato-eb] For products, see the [Midwest Vegetable Production Guide](https://mwveguide.org/) and UW–Madison's [Commercial Vegetable Production in Wisconsin (A3422)](https://vegpath.plantpath.wisc.edu/resources/a3422/); for an overview of potato pest management, see UW–Madison's [Potato Integrated Pest Management: Back to Basics](https://cropsandsoils.extension.wisc.edu/articles/potato-integrated-pest-management-back-to-basics/). For gardens, see Minnesota's [Growing potatoes in home gardens](https://extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-potatoes).

## AgWeather's models

- The [potato emergence model](/models/potato-stages) estimates the date half a planting has emerged, from degree days in air above 4 °C (39 °F) since planting. It was fitted to UW–Madison's estimated planting and emergence dates across Wisconsin's potato areas and matches them within about 3 days on average; it hasn't been checked against emergence counted in fields.[^agweather-potato-stages]
- The [early blight P-days model](/models/early-blight-pdays) and the [late blight DSV model](/models/late-blight-dsv) count from that emergence date, or from your own.
- The [Colorado potato beetle model](/models/colorado-potato-beetle) and the [aphid PVY vectors model](/models/aphid-pvy-vectors) follow these insects by degree days from January 1.
- The [potato volunteer model](/models/potato-volunteer) estimates whether tubers left in the field survived the winter, from the hours the soil spent below 27 °F at 2, 4 and 8 inches. Our soil data count more cold hours than Wisconsin's soil sensors, so the model may underrate the risk.[^agweather-potato-volunteer]
