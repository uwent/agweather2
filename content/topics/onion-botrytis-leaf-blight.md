Botrytis leaf blight, also called Botrytis leaf spot or blast, is a disease of onion and other alliums caused by the fungus _Botrytis squamosa_.[^vegpath-onion][^a3803-2004] It is a common leaf disease in the onion-growing regions of the eastern and Midwestern states, and it favors long wet periods in mild weather.[^latin-2001]

## Identification

- **Spots** are small, white and oval, up to about ¼ inch long, and may be ringed by a pale green or silvery border that looks water-soaked. They are usually the first sign of the disease.[^vegpath-onion][^latin-2001][^swett-2019]
- **Tip dieback** follows as spots multiply: leaf tips dry and wither, and many spots on a leaf can kill the whole leaf and, in severe cases, the whole top.[^vegpath-onion][^latin-2001][^swett-2019] Leaves often blight partly or wholly within 5 to 12 days after the first spots appear.[^aafc-2021]
- **Look-alike:** Botrytis leaf fleck, caused by _Botrytis cinerea_, also makes small spots, but they stay on the leaf surface, while leaf blight spots go through the leaf: slice a leaf open to tell them apart. Leaf fleck is rarely serious.[^a3803-2004]

## Life cycle

The fungus survives the winter as sclerotia, small hard resting bodies, in onion debris left in the field, in cull piles, in stored and volunteer bulbs, and in soil.[^vegpath-onion] Sclerotia can last in soil for months to years.[^swett-2019] In late spring and summer they produce spores that the wind carries to onion leaves.[^latin-2001][^aafc-2021] Spots on infected leaves then produce more spores, so the disease can spread again and again through the season.[^vegpath-onion]

Long periods of leaf wetness drive the disease. Spores form mainly at night, on dead leaf tissue that stays wet for 12 hours or more.[^aafc-2021] Mild weather favors the disease, at about 55 to 75 °F.[^latin-2001] Spores infect most readily at about 64 to 68 °F (18 to 20 °C) on leaves wet for at least 6 hours, by Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada's account; University of California guidelines say leaves must stay wet from dew or rain for 20 hours or more for spots to develop.[^aafc-2021][^swett-2019] Older leaves are more susceptible than young ones, so the disease tends to build as the season goes on; spots show first on the oldest leaves, and onions are most susceptible early in bulbing.[^latin-2001][^aafc-2021][^a3803-2004] Crowded plantings and still, humid air in the canopy also favor it.[^vegpath-onion][^swett-2019]

## Damage

When leaves die early, the bulbs stay smaller and yield drops.[^swett-2019][^vegpath-onion] Losses vary widely with the weather and the amount of disease; Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada puts them at about 7 to 30 percent.[^aafc-2021] The disease can also delay bulb maturity and lower bulb quality.[^latin-2001]

## Monitoring

- Scout fields regularly, so you find the disease early, before it spreads.[^vegpath-onion] Count spots on both the oldest and the youngest leaves: the action thresholds Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada gives are three spots per leaf on the oldest leaves, or one per leaf on the youngest.[^aafc-2021]
- Follow a weather-based forecast. BOTCAST was built to time the first fungicide spray.[^sutton-1986] UW Vegetable Pathology's guidance on its season total is: from 21 to 30, no spray is needed unless rain is predicted or you irrigate overhead; from 31, apply the first spray as soon as possible; above 40, the risk is extremely high.[^vegpath-onion]

## Management

**Nonchemical controls** reduce the sclerotia that start each season and keep leaves drier:

- Destroy cull piles and infested debris, and remove volunteer onions.[^vegpath-onion][^latin-2001]
- Rotate away from onions and other alliums for 3 years to reduce sclerotia in the soil.[^vegpath-onion][^swett-2019]
- Keep onion seed fields well away from bulb fields, since bulbs are a major source of spores.[^swett-2019][^vegpath-onion]
- Space plants so the canopy dries, and time overhead irrigation so leaves dry between waterings.[^swett-2019][^vegpath-onion]
- Avoid working in fields while plants are wet, and clean tools and machinery to limit spread.[^vegpath-onion]

No onion varieties are fully resistant, and these steps only partly control the disease, so commercial growers depend on fungicides.[^aafc-2021][^latin-2001][^a3803-2004]

**Fungicides** can be timed by scouting and a forecast rather than on a fixed schedule, which can save unneeded sprays.[^vegpath-onion][^aafc-2021]

- **Protectants** such as chlorothalonil (FRAC group M05) and mancozeb (group M03) are sprayed every 7 to 10 days, more often in weather that favors the disease.[^a3422-2026][^frac-2026]
- **Single-site fungicides** include iprodione (group 2), boscalid (group 7), pyrimethanil and cyprodinil (group 9, cyprodinil also mixed with fludioxonil, group 12), fluazinam (group 29), and strobilurins such as azoxystrobin and pyraclostrobin (group 11), often sold mixed with a fungicide from another group.[^a3422-2026] Fungicides aimed at _Botrytis_ are prone to resistance, so rotate groups and limit repeat sprays from one group.[^swett-2019][^a3422-2026] Strains resistant to iprodione have been found in onion fields in Quebec.[^aafc-2021]

For products and current registrations, see UW–Madison's [Commercial Vegetable Production in Wisconsin (A3422)](https://vegpath.plantpath.wisc.edu/resources/a3422/). UW Vegetable Pathology's [onion Botrytis page](https://vegpath.plantpath.wisc.edu/diseases/onion-botrytis/) and Purdue's [onion disease guide](https://www.extension.purdue.edu/extmedia/bp/bp-23-w.html) explain the choices in more detail; for home gardens, UW–Madison's [home vegetable garden fungicides fact sheet](https://pddc.wisc.edu/2015/07/24/home-vegetable-garden-fungicides/) lists the active ingredients for onion leaf blight.[^d0062-2026]

## AgWeather's models

The [Botrytis leaf blight model](/models/botrytis-onion-botcast) runs BOTCAST. Each day it asks whether spores are likely to be present, from recent humidity, rain and heat, and how favorable that day's wet period was for infection, and it adds the result up from crop emergence (May 15 unless you give a date). Its risk levels follow the season total: moderate above 20, high above 30 and very high above 40. Hours of high humidity stand in for leaf wetness, so fields on muck soils or under irrigation may stay wet longer than the model shows. The model reproduces the Crop Risk Tool it replaces, but it hasn't yet been compared with Botrytis leaf blight in Wisconsin onion fields.
