Oak wilt is a fatal disease of oak trees caused by the fungus _Bretziella fagacearum_. It spreads underground from tree to tree through grafted roots, and overland when small sap beetles (family Nitidulidae) carry its spores to fresh wounds. In Wisconsin it is common across the central and southern parts of the state and less common in the north.[^dnr-fact-sheet] Two sap beetles, _Colopterus truncatus_ and _Carpophilus sayi_, are its main known carriers here. These very small beetles fly mainly in spring and early summer, when a fresh wound puts an oak most at risk.[^jagemann-2018][^dnr-guidelines] Oak bark beetles can carry the fungus too.[^pddc-oak-wilt]

## Identification

- **Wilting:** leaves wilt and drop from the top of the tree first, and the symptoms move quickly down the crown. They appear from June through September, most often in July and August.[^dnr-fact-sheet]
- **Leaves** turn dull green or bronze and look water-soaked, often starting at the tips or edges. They fall while still partly green, which autumn leaves don't.[^dnr-fact-sheet][^pddc-oak-wilt]
- **Red oaks** (northern red, northern pin and black oak) are hit hardest: once they wilt, they lose most of their leaves and die within about a month. In the white oak group, bur and swamp white oaks can live several years with the disease, and white oaks decline more slowly still.[^dnr-fact-sheet]
- **Fungal mats** form under the bark of red oaks the year after they die. The mat pushes the bark out and cracks it, which lets beetles in. Mats are uncommon on bur oak and rare on white oak.[^jagemann-2018][^dnr-guidelines]
- **Look-alikes:** other pests, diseases and weather damage cause similar symptoms. Have a sample tested by a laboratory, such as UW–Madison's Plant Disease Diagnostics Clinic, before you manage a tree for oak wilt.[^dnr-fact-sheet][^dnr-oak-wilt]

## Life cycle

The fungus grows in the tree's water-conducting tissue and blocks it, so the tree wilts and dies.[^dnr-fact-sheet] Sap beetles feed on the fungal mats, pick up spores, and carry them to fresh wounds on healthy oaks or to freshly cut stumps.[^dnr-guidelines] Beetles can reach a wound within 10 minutes of its being made, and wounds and stumps can be infected for up to 72 hours.[^pddc-oak-wilt][^dnr-guidelines] From an infected tree, the fungus moves through grafted roots to nearby oaks, and a pocket of dead trees grows over the years.[^dnr-guidelines]

In a two-year trapping study in Wisconsin oak stands, _C. truncatus_ flew from March to November, peaking in April and May, and _C. sayi_ from April to November, peaking in May and July. Beetles carrying live spores were most common from May to July, and none were found after August 31.[^jagemann-2018]

The study timed the flights with [degree days](/topics/degree-days), a running total of warmth above a base temperature, here 41 °F (5 °C) from January 1. _C. truncatus_ starts early: 5 % were flying by about 231 degree days (°F) and 95 % by about 2,170. _C. sayi_ needed about 610 for 5 % and 4,220 for 95 %.[^jagemann-2018] As a rule of thumb, a week of temperatures above 60 °F is warm enough for _C. truncatus_ to emerge, so a warm March can bring the risk early.[^dnr-guidelines]

## Damage

Each year the disease kills thousands of Wisconsin's oaks, vigorous ones as well as weak, in forests, woodlots and towns.[^dnr-fact-sheet] A tree killed by oak wilt can carry spores for about a year, and moving firewood from infected trees can carry the disease to areas that were free of it.[^dnr-fact-sheet] Once oak wilt is established in a stand, it is hard and costly to control, so preventing it is the best approach.[^dnr-guidelines]

## Monitoring

- From June through September, watch oaks, especially red oaks, for wilting and leaves dropping while still partly green. Have suspect trees tested.[^dnr-fact-sheet]
- Check the Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources' map of counties with oak wilt, which it updates each year. Its harvesting guidelines apply to stands in a county with oak wilt or within six miles of one.[^dnr-guidelines]
- Follow degree days from January 1 to see when the beetles start flying where you are. The department uses them to refine when the high-risk period begins, alongside its calendar dates, not to say when it ends.[^dnr-vectors-tool]

## Management

**Nonchemical controls:**

- **Don't wound oaks while the beetles fly.** Prune only in the dormant season, from November through March, and not from April through July.[^umn-oak-wilt][^pddc-oak-wilt]
- **Harvest outside the restricted periods.** In counties with oak wilt or within six miles of one, Wisconsin's guidelines restrict harvesting from April 15 to July 15 in the north zone and from April 1 to July 15 in the south zone. They allow some exceptions for a stand, and an unusually warm March can make the risk start early.[^dnr-guidelines]
- **Cover wounds right away.** If you must prune or an oak is damaged from April through July, paint the wound at once with latex paint or shellac.[^umn-oak-wilt] On stumps cut in that period, dressing the outer three growth rings keeps beetles out.[^dnr-guidelines]
- **Break root grafts** around an infected pocket with a vibratory plow or trencher, placed correctly, then remove the dead and living oaks inside the pocket.[^dnr-fact-sheet]
- **Keep firewood local.** Cover wood from a tree that died of oak wilt with 4-mil plastic, sealed to the ground, until the bark is loose, or use it before April 1 of the next year.[^dnr-guidelines][^dnr-fact-sheet]

**Fungicide:** propiconazole, injected into the trunk by a qualified arborist, can protect a healthy oak for up to two years. It works best before infection, and on red oaks already showing symptoms it often fails. It doesn't stop spread through the roots.[^umn-oak-wilt][^pddc-oak-wilt]

For the rules on harvesting and the current county map, see Wisconsin's [Oak Harvesting Guidelines](https://embed.widencdn.net/pdf/download/widnr/aqszuho7ee/Oak-Harvesting-Guidelines-Web-version.pdf?u=lhzbht&showinbrowser=true) and the Department of Natural Resources' [oak wilt page](https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/foresthealth/oakwilt). The University of Minnesota's [oak wilt page](https://extension.umn.edu/plant-diseases/oak-wilt-minnesota) covers root graft barriers and injections in more detail; for yard trees, see UW–Madison's [oak wilt fact sheet](https://pddc.wisc.edu/2015/07/30/oak-wilt/).

## AgWeather's models

The [oak wilt vector model](/models/oak-wilt-vectors) follows the flight of _C. truncatus_ by degree days at a base of 41 °F, counted from January 1. Its levels are the study's flight percentiles for this beetle, converted to °F: Low from 180 degree days (the first beetles), Moderate from 231 (5 % flying), High from 283 (10 %) and Very high from 388 (25 %) to 913 (75 %), then High until 1,792 (90 %), Moderate until 2,172 (95 %) and Low after that. From July 15 the level drops two steps, to Low at most, and from July 22 one step more, following the end of Wisconsin's restricted period rather than the beetles. It was carried over from the earlier Wisconsin forecasting tool, VDIFN, and hasn't been compared with trap catches.[^jagemann-2018][^agweather-oak-wilt]

In AgWeather's long-term averages for 1996–2025, the first beetles come around April 17 at Arlington and April 30 at Rhinelander, and Very high runs from about May 7 to June 4 at Arlington and May 18 to June 15 at Rhinelander. The state's restricted periods start earlier, and the study caught less than 1 % of beetles before them.[^jagemann-2018]

The model leaves out _C. sayi_, which flies later: the study caught about two-thirds of them in the north and a quarter in the south after July 15, and suggested looking again at that end date, though few late beetles carried the fungus.[^jagemann-2018] Use the model alongside Wisconsin's guidelines, not instead of them.
