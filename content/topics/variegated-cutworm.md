The variegated cutworm (_Peridroma saucia_) is the caterpillar of the pearly underwing moth.[^baker-2019] It feeds on a wide range of vegetables, including asparagus, beans, carrots, celery, corn, cucumbers, lettuce, peas, peppers, potatoes, radishes and tomatoes, as well as fruit trees and ornamentals.[^umn-vcw] Like other cutworms it can cut seedlings at the soil line, but it also climbs plants to eat leaves, buds and fruit.[^umn-vcw][^umn-garden] It is also a pest of peppermint in the Pacific Northwest.[^pnw-mint] Outbreaks are sporadic and hard to predict from one year to the next.[^simonet-1981] UW Vegetable Entomology has no page of its own for it yet; its [cutworms page](https://vegento.russell.wisc.edu/pests/cutworms/) shows it among the common cutworms.[^uw-vegento]

## Identification

- **Caterpillars** are brownish gray and grow to about 2 inches. Look for a row of four to seven pale spots down the middle of the back, with darker diamond- or keyhole-shaped marks beside them. They curl up when disturbed.[^baker-2019][^umass-vcw][^pnw-mint]
- **Adults** have mottled reddish brown to grayish forewings, a wingspan of about 1¾ to 2 inches, and pale hind wings.[^baker-2019]
- **Eggs** are laid in masses of a few hundred on stems and the undersides of leaves. They are white at first and darken before they hatch.[^baker-2019][^osu-mint]

## Life cycle

Variegated cutworm moths fly into Minnesota from the south each year, and into New England between March and June.[^umn-garden][^umass-vcw] Older reports differ on the stage in which it may survive the winter.[^simonet-1981] Females lay eggs mostly on grasses and winter annual weeds, often in parts of a field that flood.[^umass-vcw] Eggs hatch in three to seven days. The caterpillars feed for three to six weeks, at night, hiding in the soil or under debris by day, then pupate in the soil.[^baker-2019][^osu-mint][^umass-vcw] There are two overlapping generations a year in Oregon's mint fields and three or four in North Carolina.[^osu-mint][^baker-2019] In Minnesota gardens the caterpillars are active from May through August or September.[^umn-vcw]

In 15 years of black light trap catches at Wooster, Ohio, moth flights peaked at about 900 and 2,160 [degree days](/topics/degree-days) counted from January 1 at a base of 45 °F with no upper limit, a generation apart.[^simonet-1981]

## Damage

- **Seedlings and transplants** may be cut at or near the soil line and killed.[^umn-vcw]
- **Larger plants:** caterpillars climb and eat foliage, buds and flowers, and bore into fruit.[^baker-2019] In mid to late summer they chew shallow or deep holes in tomatoes and other fruit.[^umass-vcw]
- **Potatoes:** caterpillars eat the foliage and can feed on tubers near the soil surface.[^berry-2000]
- **Mint:** feeding on the foliage cuts oil yield; in Oregon most damage comes in late July and early August.[^pnw-mint][^osu-mint]

## Monitoring

- DATCP's [black light traps](https://datcp.wi.gov/Pages/Programs_Services/BlackLightTraps.aspx) count variegated cutworm moths across Wisconsin, with results updated weekly through August.[^datcp-blacklight]
- Caterpillars feed at night, so check plants in the morning for fresh damage and the soil around them in the late afternoon or evening for caterpillars.[^umn-garden]
- **Mint:** from late June, shake the stems and count the caterpillars that drop onto the soil, and look in soil cracks and old curled leaves. Treatment levels run from 1 to 4 caterpillars per square foot, depending on time to harvest, natural enemies and oil price.[^pnw-mint]
- **Tomatoes:** check fruit for damage at harvest; UMass suggests treating when 1 % of plants are infested.[^umass-vcw]

## Management

**Nonchemical controls:**

- **Weeds:** weedy and reduced-till fields suffer the most damage, so controlling grasses and winter annual weeds removes egg-laying sites. Fall plowing can lower numbers where corn follows sod or hay.[^umass-vcw]
- **Natural enemies:** ground beetles, parasitic flies and wasps and other predators help keep numbers down.[^umass-vcw]
- **Home gardens:** remove debris, which exposes caterpillars to predators; collars around transplants and hand-picking also help.[^baker-2019][^umn-garden]

**Insecticides:** a rescue treatment when scouting finds caterpillars works better than a preventive soil insecticide. Because the caterpillars feed above ground at night, sprays work best applied then.[^umass-vcw] Caterpillars are easiest to kill in their second to fourth instars. The Pacific Northwest guide for mint lists chlorantraniliprole (IRAC group 28), indoxacarb (group 22A), methoxyfenozide and tebufenozide (group 18), spinosad and spinetoram (group 5), acephate (group 1B) and methomyl (group 1A), among others; _Bacillus thuringiensis_ (Bt, group 11A) products haven't worked against these cutworms in peppermint. Don't spray while bees are working blooming mint.[^pnw-mint][^irac-moa]

For products, rates and current registrations, see UW–Madison's [Commercial Vegetable Production in Wisconsin (A3422)](https://vegpath.plantpath.wisc.edu/resources/a3422/), or in neighboring states the [Midwest Vegetable Production Guide](https://mwveguide.org/guide). The [Pacific Northwest guide's mint page](https://pnwhandbooks.org/insect/agronomic/mint/mint-armyworm-cutworm) and NC State's [variegated cutworm page](https://content.ces.ncsu.edu/variegated-cutworm-on-ornamentals) explain the choices in more detail; for home gardens, see the University of Minnesota's [cutworms page](https://extension.umn.edu/yard-and-garden-insects/cutworms).

## AgWeather's models

The [variegated cutworm model](/models/variegated-cutworm) follows the Ohio study above: it counts degree days at a base of 45 °F with no upper limit from January 1, and times two moth flights peaking at about 900 and 2,160 degree days, with the caterpillars from each timed by the study's development times.[^simonet-1981][^agweather-vcw] It marks the days from each flight's peak, when the study suggests scouting for eggs and young caterpillars should begin. In AgWeather's long-term averages (1996–2025), the first flight peaks around June 14 at Arlington and June 25 at Rhinelander, and the second around August 2 and August 22.

In Wisconsin's black light traps from 2020 to 2026, the first flight's moths came somewhat later than the model's and the second's somewhat earlier, but the traps caught only 273 moths in seven years.[^datcp-blacklight][^agweather-vcw] UW Vegetable Entomology's model table lists thresholds of 41 and 88 °F,[^uw-vegento-models] which come from a model developed in Oregon peppermint; AgWeather uses the Ohio study's.[^agweather-vcw] Outbreaks are sporadic, so use trap counts and scouting to decide whether to act.
