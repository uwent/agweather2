Alternaria leaf blight is a disease of carrot leaves and leaf stalks (petioles) caused by the fungus _Alternaria dauci_. It doesn't infect the root directly, but it can kill enough of the foliage to leave roots fewer and smaller. Leaf stalks weakened by the disease snap off in mechanical harvesters, so roots are left in the field.[^uw-vegpath][^umn-diagnose][^egel-2022] It often appears on the same plants as [Cercospora leaf blight](/topics/carrot-cercospora-leaf-blight), and the two are managed together.

## Identification

- **On leaves**, spots usually start at the edges and tips of the leaflets, on the lower, older leaves first. They begin as small, greenish-brown, water-soaked spots that turn dark brown to black and are often ringed with yellow.[^cornell-2021][^umass-2013][^umn-diagnose]
- **As spots grow together**, whole leaflets wither and look scorched. Spots multiply quickly in warm, humid spells, and a badly hit field can look damaged by frost or chemical burn.[^uw-vegpath]
- **On leaf stalks**, long spots can circle the stalk and kill the whole leaf.[^uw-vegpath][^umass-2013]
- **Look-alikes:** Cercospora leaf blight is hard to tell apart in the field. It mainly attacks young, fast-growing plants, while Alternaria mainly attacks older plants, though seedlings can be infected too. Bacterial blight looks similar, but its spots are angular and stay between the leaf veins. UW–Madison's Plant Disease Diagnostics Clinic can confirm which disease you have.[^uw-vegpath]

## Life cycle

The fungus survives in infected carrot debris for up to 2 years, and on wild carrot (Queen Anne's lace). Seed can carry it, and infected seed is the main way it reaches new growing areas.[^uw-vegpath][^cornell-2021]

During the season, spores spread by wind, splashing water and field equipment. They need a film of water on the leaf to infect, from rain, irrigation, dew or high humidity. Mild to warm weather with leaves wet for a long time suits it best: infection can take place in about 8 to 12 hours of leaf wetness at 61 to 77 °F, and the warmer it is, the shorter the wet period it needs. Spots appear 3 to 5 days later, and the fungus produces new spores on the dead tissue, so the disease starts in patches and spreads across the field.[^uw-vegpath][^cornell-2021]

In Wisconsin, carrots are planted from mid-April to early June.[^a3422-2026] Because Alternaria mainly attacks older plants, it tends to matter more as the crop matures, and it is hard to reach with fungicides once the canopy closes.[^uw-vegpath][^umass-2013]

## Damage

Losses come from leaf area lost and from roots left behind at harvest. They can be considerable, and are worst when plants are infected early in the season and the disease isn't managed.[^uw-vegpath][^cornell-2021]

## Monitoring

- Each week from emergence, collect 50 leaves at random across the field and check them for spots on the leaves and leaf stalks.[^uw-vegpath]
- Walk a V- or W-shaped path with at least ten stops, and scout each variety separately.[^cornell-2021]
- Guides differ on when to start spraying: UW–Madison and the Midwest guide advise starting at the first spots, while Cornell uses 25% of leaves with at least one spot.[^uw-vegpath][^mwvg-2026][^cornell-2021]
- The Midwest guide uses a total of 15 disease severity values (DSVs, a daily score of how favorable the weather was for infection) to time sprays, and Cornell suggests spraying again once more than 15 DSVs have built up since the last spray.[^mwvg-2026][^cornell-2021]

## Management

**Nonchemical controls:**

- Plant clean seed. Hot-water seed treatment (122 °F for 20 minutes) can reduce seedborne infection.[^mwvg-2026][^umass-2013]
- Rotate out of carrots for at least 2 to 3 years, and work crop debris into the soil promptly after harvest so it breaks down.[^uw-vegpath][^mwvg-2026][^cornell-2021]
- Choose tolerant varieties; several are available.[^uw-vegpath][^cornell-2021] In UW–Madison trials at Hancock in 2022 and 2024, which rated Alternaria and Cercospora together, Naval, Belgrado and Canberra were among the varieties with the least leaf blight.[^uw-carrot-trial-2022][^uw-carrot-trial-2024]
- Plant in well-drained fields, and irrigate early in the day so leaves dry quickly.[^uw-vegpath] Keep plants vigorous with good fertility and avoid injuring them.[^cornell-2021]

**Fungicides** work best started early: begin when you first find spots, or when a forecast calls for a spray.[^uw-vegpath][^mwvg-2026]

- **Multisite fungicides:** chlorothalonil (FRAC group M05), applied at the first sign of infection, and copper (group M01), which organic growers and home gardeners can use.[^a3422-2026][^mwvg-2026][^egel-2022][^gevens-xht1211]
- **Single-site fungicides:** quinone outside inhibitors (QoIs, group 11; azoxystrobin, pyraclostrobin, trifloxystrobin), succinate dehydrogenase inhibitors (SDHIs, group 7; boscalid, penthiopyrad, fluxapyroxad, fluopyram, pydiflumetofen), demethylation inhibitors (DMIs, group 3; difenoconazole, propiconazole), anilinopyrimidines (group 9; cyprodinil, pyrimethanil) and iprodione (group 2). Labels limit how many sprays of one group may be made in a row; alternate groups to slow the spread of resistant strains.[^a3422-2026][^mwvg-2026][^cornell-2021]

For products, rates and current registrations, see [Commercial Vegetable Production in Wisconsin (A3422)](https://vegpath.plantpath.wisc.edu/resources/a3422/), or in neighboring states the [Midwest Vegetable Production Guide](https://mwveguide.org/guide). UW–Madison's [carrot leaf blight page](https://vegpath.plantpath.wisc.edu/diseases/carrot-alternaria-and-cercospora-leaf-blights/) and Cornell's [carrot leaf blight fact sheet](https://vegetables.cornell.edu/pest-management/disease-factsheets/carrot-leaf-blight-diseases-and-their-management) explain the choices in more detail; for home gardens, see UW–Madison's [home vegetable garden fungicides](https://vegpath.plantpath.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/342/2021/02/XHT1211-Home-Vegetable-Garden-Fungicides.pdf).

## AgWeather's models

The [carrot foliar blight model](/models/carrot-foliar-dsv), which also serves Cercospora leaf blight, gives each day 0 to 4 DSVs from the hours of high humidity and the mean temperature during them, using the TOMCAST method. It rates risk from the total over the last 7 days, and also shows the total since planting or your last fungicide spray, which you can compare with the 15 DSVs the guides use. We take high humidity as a stand-in for leaf wetness rather than measuring it. TOMCAST was developed for early blight of tomato, and we haven't yet compared the model's risk levels with leaf blight in Wisconsin carrot fields.[^agweather-carrot-foliar]
