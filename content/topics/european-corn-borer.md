The European corn borer (_Ostrinia nubilalis_) is a moth whose caterpillars bore into the stalks, ear shanks and ears of field and sweet corn.[^iowa-ecb][^uw-vegento] It also attacks snap beans, peppers and potatoes.[^cornell-ecb] Before Bt corn it was among the costliest corn pests, known as "the billion-dollar bug." Since Bt hybrids came into wide use after 1996, its numbers have been held low across large areas.[^iowa-ecb][^umn-ecb] Corn without Bt protection, sweet corn and vegetable crops still need scouting.[^umn-ecb][^uw-vegento]

## Identification

- **Adults** are straw-colored moths with a wingspan of about 1 inch. Males are a little smaller and darker than females.[^uw-vegento]
- **Eggs** are laid in flat masses of about 20 to 30 that overlap like fish scales, on the undersides of leaves near the midrib. They turn from white to cream. Just before they hatch, the caterpillars' black heads show through: the "black-head" stage.[^uw-vegento]
- **Caterpillars** are gray to creamy white with a black head and many dark spots, and grow to ¾ to 1 inch.[^uw-vegento]

## Life cycle

Corn borers spend the winter as full-grown caterpillars inside corn stalks, weed stems and vegetable stems left in the field. They start developing again once temperatures pass 50 °F, pupate in May, and the moths appear in June.[^uw-vegento] There are two generations a year in most of Wisconsin, but only one in the northern counties. In southern Wisconsin the first eggs are laid from late May to mid-June.[^flandermeyer-2025] In eastern and northern Wisconsin, where Lake Michigan cools the spring, the flight can run into July.[^uw-vegento]

Moths lay eggs on warm, calm evenings, and the eggs hatch in about six days in late June in central Wisconsin. The young caterpillars feed in the whorl and by their third stage tunnel into the leaf midribs and then the stalk. They are full grown in about three weeks. Heavy rain before they bore in kills many of them.[^uw-vegento]

Caterpillars that mature early in the season pupate in the stalk, and their moths fly from late July or early August.[^flandermeyer-2025] This summer flight peaks around mid-August in central Wisconsin. Caterpillars that mature later, as most in northern Wisconsin do, stay in the stalk until spring.[^uw-vegento]

## Damage

- **Field corn:** stalk tunnels cut yield, and second-generation tunnels break stalks and drop ears before harvest.[^uw-vegento] Wisconsin's worksheets count about 5 % yield loss for each first-generation borer per plant and 4 % for each second-generation borer.[^a3646-2025]
- **Sweet corn:** the main loss is in the ears. Processors must trim damaged ears, and shoppers reject a fresh-market ear with a borer in it. Most ear damage comes from infestations that begin once most plants have tasseled.[^uw-vegento]
- **Peppers and beans:** caterpillars bore into stems, pepper fruit and bean pods, leaving pinhole entries plugged with droppings (frass). In potatoes they mostly bore into stems in the first generation.[^illinois-ecb]

## Monitoring

- Wisconsin's Department of Agriculture, Trade and Consumer Protection (DATCP) posts weekly [black light trap counts](https://datcp.wi.gov/Pages/Programs_Services/BlackLightTraps.aspx) of corn borer moths through the summer.[^datcp-blacklight] Trap counts show when moths are flying, but they don't replace scouting.[^flandermeyer-2025]
- **Field corn, first generation:** once corn is 18 inches tall, check 20 plants in a row in each of five areas for pinholes in the whorl leaves. Unroll the whorls of two damaged plants per area and count live caterpillars, then use UW–Madison's worksheet to see whether a spray will pay: in field corn it doesn't always.[^flandermeyer-2025][^a3646-2025][^uw-vegento]
- **Field corn, second generation:** egg laying runs over several weeks, so check late-planted fields weekly for egg masses on the undersides of leaves near the ear and above it.[^a3646-2025]
- **Fresh-market sweet corn:** in southern Wisconsin, start checking for egg masses by June 15. Through early silk, treat if 5 % of plants have egg masses, caterpillars or leaf feeding.[^uw-vegento][^flandermeyer-2025]
- **Processing sweet corn:** treat in the whorl stage if 25 % of plants are infested, and from late tassel to silk at 4 to 5 %.[^uw-vegento]
- UW–Madison times control of first-generation caterpillars at about 800 to 1,000 degree days (base 50 °F), mid-June to early July in southern Wisconsin, and of the second generation at about 1,500 to 2,100, just before eggs hatch.[^flandermeyer-2025]

## Management

**Nonchemical controls:**

- **Natural controls:** weather, predators, parasitic insects and diseases can kill 80 to 90 % of eggs and young caterpillars, and UW–Madison credits them with the 5 to 7 years of low numbers that usually separate outbreaks. Spraying only when needed conserves them.[^uw-vegento]
- **Crop residue:** cutting corn for silage, shredding stalks and plowing under stubble kill many overwintering borers; dry-stalk shredding may kill 80 %. Plowing raises the risk of soil erosion, though.[^a3646-2025]
- **Bt corn:** field and sweet corn hybrids with Bt proteins control corn borers for the life of the plant.[^cornell-ecb][^a3646-2025] Federal rules require a refuge of non-Bt corn, 5 to 20 % depending on the product, often mixed in the bag.[^a3646-2025][^iowa-ecb] Resistant corn borers have been found in Canada and Connecticut, so scout Bt fields too.[^iowa-ecb]
- **Bt sprays** (_Bacillus thuringiensis_; IRAC group 11A) kill young caterpillars, spare natural enemies and include products approved for organic use, but last only a few days.[^a3646-2025][^irac-moa]

**Insecticides** work only before caterpillars bore into the plant: for the first generation, while they are in the whorl, and for the second, when most egg masses are at the black-head stage or starting to hatch.[^a3646-2025] UW–Madison's field corn guide lists pyrethroids (group 3A, such as lambda-cyhalothrin and bifenthrin), diamides (group 28, chlorantraniliprole) and carbamates (group 1A, carbaryl), as well as Bt.[^a3646-2025]

For products, rates and current registrations, see UW–Madison's [Commercial Vegetable Production in Wisconsin (A3422)](https://vegpath.plantpath.wisc.edu/resources/a3422/) for sweet corn and vegetables and [Pest Management in Wisconsin Field Crops (A3646)](https://cropsandsoils.extension.wisc.edu/files/2025/01/A3646_2025_web.pdf) for field corn, or in neighboring states the [Midwest Vegetable Production Guide](https://mwveguide.org/guide). UW Vegetable Entomology's [European corn borer page](https://vegento.russell.wisc.edu/pests/european-corn-borer/) and UW–Madison's [corn borer article](https://cropsandsoils.extension.wisc.edu/articles/managing-european-corn-borers-in-wisconsin-corn-fields) explain the choices in more detail; for home gardens, see the University of Illinois's [European corn borer page](https://extension.illinois.edu/gardening/european-corn-borer).

## AgWeather's models

The [European corn borer model](/models/european-corn-borer) counts degree days from January 1 by the modified average method (base 50 °F, cutoff 86 °F), as UW–Madison's degree-day table does.[^uw-dd-table] It follows the spring moth flight from about 374 degree days (peaking at 631), the egg masses and young caterpillars that come from it, and a summer flight about 1,100 degree days later (peaking near 1,733), and marks the days from 10 % to 90 % of each generation's egg hatch as the time to scout and treat young caterpillars before they bore in. That window opens a little before UW–Madison's spray timings of 800 to 1,000 and about 1,500 to 2,100 degree days.[^flandermeyer-2025]

On DATCP's black light trap counts from 2020 to 2026, the middle of each flight fell within a day or so of the observed flights in southern and central Wisconsin.[^agweather-ecb][^datcp-blacklight] In the north, where most corn borers have one generation, Wausau's trap caught a single flight from mid-July to mid-August, between the model's two flights, so the model doesn't fit there yet.[^agweather-ecb][^uw-vegento] Minnesota's guide also times the one-generation strain later than the spring flight of the two-generation strain.[^umn-ecb] Time scouting by trap catches in your area and egg masses in your fields.
