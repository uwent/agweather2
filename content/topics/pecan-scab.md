Pecan scab is a disease of pecan caused by the fungus _Venturia effusa_, which older publications call _Cladosporium caryigenum_. It is the most damaging disease of pecans in the southeastern United States and across most of the crop's range.[^standish-2021][^osu-epp7642] It infects young leaves, green twigs and nut shucks in wet weather, and it can cost a susceptible variety most of its crop.[^msu-p3966][^uada-scab] AgWeather's model for it runs only in the southern pecan-growing states, not in Wisconsin.

## Identification

- **On leaves, twigs and shucks,** scab makes small, dark spots, olive to black, round or elongated and often fuzzy. Spots can merge into large blackened patches.[^osu-epp7642][^msu-p3966]
- **On nuts,** infection of the shuck causes most of the losses. Nuts infected early can drop before they mature.[^uada-scab] A pink mold can grow in old scab lesions on shucks and, if it reaches the kernel, give it an off flavor.[^osu-epp7642]
- **Look-alike:** vein spot, another fungal leaf disease, makes spots like scab's, but they are long and follow the veins, and it doesn't attack shoots or nuts.[^osu-epp7642]

## Life cycle

The fungus spends the winter in the tree, in lesions on twigs and nut shucks from the year before.[^standish-2021][^osu-epp7642] In spring these produce spores that infect leaves, twigs and shucks during long rainy periods or heavy dews. New lesions then produce more spores, and the cycle repeats through the summer.[^osu-epp7642][^standish-2021]

The fungus infects only young, actively growing tissue. Pecans have such tissue through most of the season, until the nuts stop growing in the fall. Infection needs moisture, from rain or heavy fog,[^msu-p3966] and is most likely during long spells of leaf wetness, when relative humidity stays above 90 % and the temperature above 70 °F.[^uada-scab]

## Damage

Scab can strip leaves, slow shoot growth and destroy nuts; a severe outbreak can take the whole crop.[^msu-p3966] Without management, susceptible varieties can lose 50 to 100 % of their crop.[^uada-scab] Losing leaves early also hurts the next year: trees need healthy leaves late into the fall to finish this year's nuts, and trees that lost their leaves the season before produce few nuts.[^osu-epp7642] Fungicides can be a commercial grower's largest single operating cost.[^standish-2021]

## Monitoring

- **Know your varieties.** The Oklahoma Mesonet groups them as highly susceptible (such as Desirable, Pawnee and Wichita), moderately susceptible (such as Caddo, Kiowa and Stuart) and of low susceptibility (such as Excel, Kanza and Lakota).[^mesonet-advisor]
- **Track scab hours.** The Mesonet's [Pecan Scab Advisor](https://www.mesonet.org/agriculture/horticulture/pecan/pecan-scab-advisor) counts hours with relative humidity of 90 % or more and a temperature of 70 °F or more, from March 1 to August 31. It recommends a spray once 10 scab hours have built up for highly susceptible varieties, 20 for moderately susceptible ones and 30 for those of low susceptibility, and once 30 days have passed since March 1, or the protection period you choose (5 to 14 days) since the last spray.[^mesonet-advisor]
- **Scout often** for spots on leaves and shucks, and keep an eye on the weather.[^uada-scab]

## Management

**Nonchemical controls:**

- **Plant resistant varieties.** This is the best single control.[^osu-epp7642] Resistance may not last: a pecan tree can live 100 years or more, longer than its resistance may hold.[^msu-p3966]
- **Let air and light in.** Remove low limbs and crowded trees so the canopy dries faster, and keep drainage channels open so water leaves the orchard quickly.[^osu-epp7642]
- **Sanitation:** remove fallen branches from the orchard floor.[^uada-scab]

**Fungicides** protect healthy tissue rather than cure infections, so apply them before or shortly after wet periods.[^msu-p3966][^osu-epp7642] In Oklahoma, a dry season may need only two or three sprays and a wet one as many as six.[^osu-epp7642] In Georgia's humid climate, sprays go on every 10 to 14 days before pollination and every 10 to 21 days after it, depending on the weather, and continue through the summer.[^uga-c1174]

- **Classes used** include demethylation inhibitors (DMIs, FRAC group 3; propiconazole), a quinone outside inhibitor with a succinate dehydrogenase inhibitor (QoI and SDHI, groups 11 and 7; pyraclostrobin and boscalid), phosphorous acid (group P07) and ziram (group M03). Ziram gives fair to good control with a low risk of resistance; sulfur (group M02) generally controls scab poorly.[^msu-p3966][^frac-2026]
- **Resistance** is a serious problem. Since the 1970s, the fungus has become resistant or less sensitive to benzimidazoles (FRAC group 1), DMIs (group 3), QoIs (group 11), organotin fungicides (group 30) and guanidines (group U12).[^standish-2021][^frac-2026] Rotate FRAC groups from spray to spray; products that combine two groups lower the risk further.[^msu-p3966]
- **Home trees:** spraying a tall tree well takes orchard equipment, so a fungicide program is rarely practical for a homeowner. Choose resistant varieties suited to your area, and give trees sun and airflow.[^msu-p3966]

For products and current registrations, see your state's pecan spray guide, such as the [Arkansas Plant Disease Control Products Guide](https://uaex.uada.edu/publications/mp-154.aspx) (MP154). Oklahoma State's [pecan diseases fact sheet](https://pods.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/EPP-7642pod2013.pdf) and the Oklahoma Mesonet's [Pecan Scab Advisor](https://www.mesonet.org/agriculture/horticulture/pecan/pecan-scab-advisor) explain spray timing in more detail; for home trees, see Mississippi State's [pecan scab publication](https://extension.msstate.edu/publications/pecan-scab-the-home-landscape).

## AgWeather's models

The [pecan scab model](/models/pecan-scab) follows the Oklahoma Mesonet's advisor. It counts scab hours, with relative humidity of 90 % or more and a temperature of 70 °F or more, from March 1 or the date of your last fungicide spray, through August 31, and it shows the last 14 days' total too. You choose your variety's susceptibility, which sets the threshold (10, 20 or 30 scab hours), and the days a spray protects (5, 7, 10 or 14). Risk is High, meaning a spray is advised, once the total reaches the threshold and the waiting period has passed: 30 days after March 1, or the protection period after your last spray. It is Moderate from half the threshold, or at the threshold while the waiting period lasts, and Low below half.[^mesonet-advisor][^agweather-pecan-scab]

The model runs at locations in 16 pecan-growing states, from the Carolinas and Florida west to California, with weather from Open-Meteo, because AgWeather's own maps don't reach the South. It was carried over from the Crop Protection Network's Crop Risk Tool, whose code counted hours at 92 % humidity and above 21.1 °C (70 °F); we count at the Mesonet's 90 % and 70 °F. Its humidity hasn't been compared with weather stations across the South (at one Oklahoma station in 2025 it ran drier than the station's, so it may count fewer scab hours), and its scab hours haven't been compared with the Mesonet's advisor or with scab in orchards.[^agweather-pecan-scab]
