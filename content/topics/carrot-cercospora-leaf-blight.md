Cercospora leaf blight is a disease of carrot leaves and leaf stalks (petioles) caused by the fungus _Cercospora carotae_. It usually appears together with [Alternaria leaf blight](/topics/carrot-alternaria-leaf-blight), and the two foliar blights occur every year in Wisconsin.[^rogers-2006] Neither infects the root. The loss comes when blighted, brittle petioles break off in the mechanical harvester and leave the carrots in the ground.[^uw-vegpath-carrot]

## Identification

- **On leaves**, spots start as small, greenish-brown, water-soaked flecks, often with a yellow halo. They become cream to gray with a distinct dark border: round inside the leaflet, longer along its edge. When the air stays humid, spores form on the lower side of the spots, which then look pale gray with tiny black dots.[^uw-vegpath-carrot][^cornell-carrot-2021]
- **On petioles**, spots are elliptical with tan or lighter centers.[^uw-vegpath-carrot][^cornell-carrot-2021]
- **As it spreads**, spots run together and whole leaflets shrivel and die.[^cornell-carrot-2021]
- **Telling it from Alternaria:** Cercospora spots are common on young leaves as well as old ones, while Alternaria's dark brown to black, irregular spots start on the lower, older leaves. Cercospora tends to strike young, fast-growing plants, Alternaria older ones, but the two are hard to tell apart and often share a plant. Bacterial blight makes angular spots held between the veins.[^uw-vegpath-carrot][^cornell-carrot-2021]

## Life cycle

The fungus spends the winter in infected carrot debris, where it can survive up to 2 years, and on wild carrot (Queen Anne's lace). It also travels on seed, the main way it reaches new fields. Once the debris has broken down, it does not last long in the soil.[^uw-vegpath-carrot][^cornell-carrot-2021]

During the season, wind, splashing water and equipment carry spores from plant to plant. Infection needs long periods of wet leaves: at least 12 hours at 68 to 86 °F. New spots can then appear in 3 to 5 days, so the disease builds quickly in warm, wet stretches. Since its spots show up on young leaves, it can do more damage early in the season, while plants still have few leaves.[^uw-vegpath-carrot][^cornell-carrot-2021]

## Damage

Heavy blight kills leaves, and badly damaged plants may have smaller roots.[^umn-carrot] Blighted petioles weaken and snap when the harvester lifts the tops, so roots are left behind. Losses are worst when plants are infected early.[^uw-vegpath-carrot]

## Monitoring

- Each week from emergence, collect 50 leaves at random across the field and look for spots on leaves and petioles. Walk a V or W pattern with at least 10 stops, and count a leaf as infected if it has even one spot.[^uw-vegpath-carrot][^cornell-carrot-2021]
- Scout each cultivar separately, since they differ in how quickly they become infected.[^cornell-carrot-2021]
- UW–Madison and the Midwest guide start fungicides when the first spots appear. Cornell uses a threshold of 25 % of leaves infected. In Michigan trials, waiting until 5 to 10 % of the foliage was blighted often gave poorer control than starting at the first trace.[^uw-vegpath-carrot][^midwest-veg-guide-2026][^cornell-carrot-2021][^bounds-2007]

## Management

**Nonchemical controls:**

- Buy clean seed; hot water treatment (122 °F for 20 minutes) can reduce seedborne infection.[^midwest-veg-guide-2026]
- Plant tolerant cultivars. In Wisconsin trials, the more resistant cultivar Bolero needed fewer sprays than a susceptible one to control both blights.[^rogers-2006][^uw-vegpath-carrot]
- Rotate away from carrot for at least 2 to 3 years, and work crop debris into the soil soon after harvest.[^uw-vegpath-carrot][^cornell-carrot-2021]
- Help leaves dry: choose well-drained fields, irrigate early in the day, and consider wider rows or raised beds.[^uw-vegpath-carrot][^cornell-carrot-2021]

**Fungicides:**

- **Protectants**: chlorothalonil (FRAC group M05) and copper (group M01). Copper is the option for organic growers; in field trials it controlled the blights, though less well than the other programs tested. Home gardeners can use chlorothalonil or copper.[^a3422-2026][^dorman-2009][^gevens-xht1211]
- **Single-site fungicides**: quinone outside inhibitors (QoIs, group 11; azoxystrobin, pyraclostrobin, trifloxystrobin), succinate dehydrogenase inhibitors (SDHIs, group 7; penthiopyrad, fluxapyroxad, pydiflumetofen) and demethylation inhibitors (DMIs, group 3; propiconazole, difenoconazole), alone or in premixes. A few of the fungicides in Wisconsin's carrot leaf blight table, such as boscalid and iprodione, are labeled for Alternaria only. Rotate groups: for most group 11 products, Wisconsin's guide allows only one application before switching to another group.[^a3422-2026][^midwest-veg-guide-2026]
- **Timing by weather**: TOMCAST scores each day's weather in disease severity values (DSVs). In Wisconsin trials, spraying after each 15 to 20 DSVs cut fungicide use by 30 to 50 % from weekly sprays without losing control or yield; Michigan trials confirmed a 15-DSV program started at the first trace.[^rogers-2006][^bounds-2007]

For products, rates and current registrations, see UW–Madison's [Commercial Vegetable Production in Wisconsin (A3422)](https://vegpath.plantpath.wisc.edu/resources/a3422/), or in neighboring states the [Midwest Vegetable Production Guide](https://mwveguide.org/guide). UW–Madison's [carrot leaf blight page](https://vegpath.plantpath.wisc.edu/diseases/carrot-alternaria-and-cercospora-leaf-blights/) and Cornell's [carrot leaf blight fact sheet](https://vegetables.cornell.edu/pest-management/disease-factsheets/carrot-leaf-blight-diseases-and-their-management) explain the choices in more detail; for home gardens, see UW–Madison's [home vegetable garden fungicides](https://vegpath.plantpath.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/342/2021/02/XHT1211-Home-Vegetable-Garden-Fungicides.pdf).

## AgWeather's models

The [carrot foliar blight model](/models/carrot-foliar-dsv), which also serves Alternaria leaf blight, uses the TOMCAST method: it gives each day 0 to 4 disease severity values from the hours of high humidity and their mean temperature. Its risk level comes from the total over the last 7 days. It also shows the total since planting or your last spray, which you can compare with the 15-DSV spray interval that the Midwest guide and Cornell use. We take high humidity as a stand-in for leaf wetness rather than measuring it. TOMCAST was developed for early blight of tomato, and we haven't yet compared the model's risk levels with leaf blight in Wisconsin carrot fields.[^agweather-carrot-foliar-dsv][^cornell-carrot-2021][^midwest-veg-guide-2026]
