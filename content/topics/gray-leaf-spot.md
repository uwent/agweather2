Gray leaf spot is a leaf disease of corn caused by the fungus _Cercospora zeae-maydis_, whose only known host is corn.[^malvick-2018] It is common in southern Wisconsin, and how much damage it does depends on the weather and the hybrid's susceptibility.[^a3646-2025][^cpn-gls] In Ohio it caused heavy losses in the late 1990s and early 2000s; more resistant hybrids have made it less of a problem since, though it still causes losses in fields where conditions favor it.[^salgado-2016]

## Identification

- **Early lesions** are small yellow to tan spots, often with a faint halo you can see when you hold the leaf up to the light.[^salgado-2016][^malvick-2018]
- **Mature lesions** are long, narrow and rectangular: about 1/16 to 1/8 inch wide and up to 2 to 4 inches long, with straight, parallel sides because the leaf veins limit them. They are tan to brown, turn gray when the fungus produces spores, and look opaque against the light. The straight edges help tell gray leaf spot from northern corn leaf spot.[^salgado-2016][^malvick-2018][^cpn-gls]
- **Severe disease:** lesions merge and can kill whole leaves. Some hybrids show yellow or orange lesions, and in hot, dry weather lesions may stay as yellowish flecks without clear edges.[^salgado-2016][^cpn-gls]

## Life cycle

The fungus spends the winter in corn residue on the soil surface. In late spring, warm, humid weather triggers spores on the residue, and wind and splashing rain carry them to the lower leaves of the new crop.[^salgado-2016][^cpn-gls] Infection needs long, unbroken spells of moisture: 11 to 13 hours of dew or fog, with humidity in the canopy at or above 90 % for 12 to 13 hours without a break; Minnesota's guidance gives more than 24 hours above 95 %.[^salgado-2016][^malvick-2018] Lesions grow fastest and produce the most spores at 77 to 86 °F in humid weather.[^salgado-2016]

Symptoms usually appear first on the lower leaves two to three weeks before tasseling. Spores from these lesions then spread the disease up the plant, and in susceptible hybrids it develops mostly from silking to maturity.[^cpn-gls][^malvick-2018] The earlier infection starts, the more time the disease has to spread and the more it costs.[^salgado-2016]

Gray leaf spot is worse where corn follows corn and residue stays on the surface, as in no-till and reduced tillage, and on susceptible hybrids.[^a3646-2025][^malvick-2018] Low ground and river bottoms, late planting and weedy fields all keep the canopy humid longer.[^salgado-2016]

## Damage

Lesions destroy the green leaf area that fills the grain, so yield falls as the disease climbs the plant during grain fill. Where conditions favor the disease, losses of 5 to 40 bushels per acre are possible. Severely diseased plants can die early, which lowers silage value and makes stalk rot and lodging more likely.[^salgado-2016]

## Monitoring

- Scout fields of susceptible hybrids weekly from about V10 to V14 (10 to 14 leaf collars), and again from tasseling to milk, when a fungicide decision matters most.[^salgado-2016][^a3646-2025]
- Check the lower leaves first, where symptoms appear earliest,[^cpn-gls] then note whether lesions are reaching the leaves at and above the ear. Wet weather or heavy dews on a field that already shows lesions signal a rising risk.[^a3646-2025]
- If the disease already covers much of the leaf area above the ear, a fungicide will do little.[^a3646-2025]

## Management

**Nonchemical controls** are the base of management:

- Plant hybrids with good resistance where gray leaf spot has been a problem. Resistance is partial: it slows the disease rather than stopping it. Ask your seed supplier for ratings and check local hybrid trials.[^a3646-2025][^salgado-2016]
- Rotate away from corn and manage residue. Where a field has a history of the disease, a year out of corn with tillage that buries residue, or two years out of corn under reduced tillage, cuts the fungus's survival.[^salgado-2016][^malvick-2018]

**Fungicides** can reduce gray leaf spot, but for field corn they rarely pay unless the disease is active between tasseling and milk on a susceptible hybrid. Seed corn growers spray more often, because inbreds can be very susceptible.[^a3646-2025] A fungicide is most likely to pay on a susceptible hybrid with high yield potential, in humid weather, with lesions already present.[^malvick-2018]

- **Timing:** applications from tasseling (VT) to brown silk (R2) have given the best control and yield response; sprays at V4 to V10 are less effective.[^salgado-2016]
- **Classes:** quinone outside inhibitors (QoIs, FRAC group 11; azoxystrobin, pyraclostrobin) and some demethylation inhibitors (DMIs, group 3; tetraconazole) rate excellent against gray leaf spot, and many premixes combine these groups with succinate dehydrogenase inhibitors (SDHIs, group 7).[^cpn-efficacy-2026]
- **Restrictions:** check harvest intervals on the label, which run from 7 to more than 30 days and may differ for silage.[^cpn-efficacy-2026]

For products and current registrations, see UW–Madison's [Pest Management in Wisconsin Field Crops (A3646)](https://cropsandsoils.extension.wisc.edu/files/2025/01/A3646_2025_web.pdf) and the Corn Disease Working Group's [fungicide efficacy ratings](https://cropprotectionnetwork.org/publications/fungicide-efficacy-for-control-of-corn-diseases). Ohio State's [Gray Leaf Spot of Corn](https://ohioline.osu.edu/factsheet/plpath-cer-05) and Minnesota's [gray leaf spot page](https://extension.umn.edu/corn-pest-management/gray-leaf-spot-corn) explain the choices in more detail.

## AgWeather's models

The [gray leaf spot model](/models/gray-leaf-spot) gives a probability from the last three weeks' mean nightly low temperature and the last month's mean daily minimum dew point. It is shown from V10 (ten leaf collars) to milk, using the [corn stages model](/models/corn-stages), or your own planting date at a location.[^agweather-gray-leaf-spot]

Read it with caution. Its equation comes from the Crop Risk Tool, credited to UW–Madison and North Dakota State researchers, and hasn't been published, so we don't know where or when it was fitted or whether it predicts spores or disease. With nightly lows near 59 °F, it reaches High once the month's mean minimum dew point passes about 43 °F, so it is likely to read High on most days of a Wisconsin summer; in a check over one summer at Eau Claire, it was High on every day from July 1 to August 15. Warmer nights also lower its probability slightly, though warm weather favors the disease. It can remind you that the season favors the disease, but it can't tell one week, field or year from another, so time sprays by scouting. Our version reproduces the Crop Risk Tool exactly, but it hasn't been compared with gray leaf spot in Wisconsin fields.[^agweather-gray-leaf-spot]
