Eggs of the common stalk borer (_Paipanema nebris_), a cutworm relative, are laid in grassy weeds in September. In late May to July, the brown-and-white striped caterpillars migrate into fields and burrow into the stems of tomatoes, potatoes, beans, and other thick-stemmed plants. Once inside the stem, the insect cannot be controlled. Fall grass control will prevent egg laying and is the best control method for common stalk borer.
