Spring wheat (_Triticum aestivum_) is wheat sown in spring and harvested in late summer, unlike winter wheat, which is sown in autumn. It is a minor crop in Wisconsin, where nearly all the wheat grown is winter wheat, but a major one to the north and west.[^nass-wi-stats][^nass-small-grains-2025] Its leaf, heading and flowering stages time herbicides, fungicides and the decision on [Fusarium head blight (scab)](/topics/wheat-scab), so this page describes those stages and the scales used to name them.

## Spring wheat in Wisconsin

Wisconsin's wheat statistics count winter wheat alone: in recent years the state has harvested about 220,000 to 250,000 acres of it.[^nass-wi-stats] The U.S. Department of Agriculture estimates spring wheat (other than durum) in six states, Idaho, Minnesota, Montana, North Dakota, South Dakota and Washington, and North Dakota plants about half the nation's acres.[^nass-small-grains-2025] Most guidance on growing spring wheat therefore comes from Minnesota and North Dakota.

Plant spring wheat as early as you can. Cool weather between about the four- and five-and-a-half-leaf stages gives more spikelets per head, and much of the yield potential is set by the six-leaf stage. In Minnesota and North Dakota, yields fall by about 1 % for each day planting is delayed past the best date, which ranges from the first week of April in southern Minnesota to the first week of May near the Canadian border.[^wiersma-planting] Young plants tolerate frost well: until about the five-leaf stage the growing point is about an inch below the soil surface, and the crown survives temperatures down to 28 °F.[^wiersma-planting] Early planting also helps spring wheat escape stem rust and leaf rust, and the barley yellow dwarf virus carried by aphids.[^a3646-2025]

## Growth stages

Three scales are in common use. The Feekes scale numbers the main stages from 1 to 11; the Zadoks scale uses two digits, the first for the main stage and the second for steps within it; and the Haun scale counts leaves, giving each growing leaf as a fraction of the one before it, so that 3.2 means three leaves are fully out and the fourth is a fifth as long as the third.[^bongard-stages] The main stages, with their Zadoks and Feekes numbers:[^bongard-stages]

- **Emergence** (Zadoks 10, Feekes 1): the first leaf comes through the coleoptile. Leaves then appear about every 4 to 5 days, eight or nine on the main stem.
- **Tillering** (Zadoks 20–25, Feekes 2–3): side shoots grow from the base. The head starts to form, still microscopic.
- **Jointing** (Zadoks 31, Feekes 6): the first node can be felt on the stem.
- **Flag leaf** (Zadoks 37, Feekes 8): the last leaf is just visible.
- **Boot** (Zadoks 45, Feekes 10): the head swells inside the flag leaf sheath.
- **Heading** (Zadoks 51–59, Feekes 10.1–10.5): the head emerges.
- **Flowering** (Zadoks 61–65, Feekes 10.5.1–10.5.2): from the first flowers to half the florets; a head flowers for about 4 days.
- **Grain fill:** milk (Zadoks 71–75), soft dough (Zadoks 85, Feekes 11.2) and physiological maturity (about Zadoks 89, at 30 to 40 % kernel moisture), about four weeks in all. The crop is ready to combine at 13 to 14 % moisture.

Stages follow warmth, so [degree days](/topics/degree-days) from planting predict them. The method North Dakota uses counts from a base of 32 °F, caps the daily high at 70 °F until the second leaf is out and at 95 °F after, and puts emergence at about 180 degree days, a new leaf every 143, and the start of flowering at about 1,739.[^ndawn-gdd] Emergence is the least certain step, since planting depth, soil moisture and residue all change it, and stress can leave plants with only seven leaves on the main stem.[^ndawn-gdd]

## What the stages mean for pests and diseases

- **Leaf stages:** seedling leaf numbers are used to time herbicide applications.[^bongard-stages]
- **Flag leaf to boot:** fungicides for Septoria leaf blotch go on now to protect the flag leaf. Rusts cost the most yield when they infect before the head emerges.[^a3646-2025]
- **Flowering:** the upper leaves and the head supply most of the grain's carbohydrate, and losing leaves at flowering costs more yield than losing them later; in one study, removing leaves at flowering cut kernel weight by 20 %, against 7 % two weeks later.[^bongard-stages]
- **Flowering (Feekes 10.5.1) to about 7 days after:** wheat is most susceptible to [Fusarium head blight (scab)](/topics/wheat-scab), and fungicides for it work best in this window. Fungicides in the strobilurin group (FRAC 11) applied at boot or later can raise the toxin DON in the grain.[^friskop-2021]

## Monitoring

- Record each field's planting date and variety, including its scab resistance rating. Degree-day stages and scab risk depend on both.[^ndawn-gdd][^kriss-2016]
- Confirm flowering by looking at several heads in several places in the field, rather than trusting a date.[^kriss-2016]

## Management

Plant early, choose varieties with good disease resistance, and rotate away from wheat and corn to reduce scab.[^wiersma-planting][^friskop-2021] For products, see UW–Madison's [Pest Management in Wisconsin Field Crops (A3646)](https://cropsandsoils.extension.wisc.edu/files/2025/01/A3646_2025_web.pdf); for stages and planting dates, see the University of Minnesota's [Spring wheat growth and development guide](https://extension.umn.edu/growing-small-grains/spring-wheat-growth-and-development-guide) and [planting date](https://extension.umn.edu/planting-small-grains/planting-date) pages.

## AgWeather's models

- The [spring wheat stages model](/models/spring-wheat-stages) estimates each field's Haun stage from planting through flowering to watery-ripe kernels, using North Dakota's degree-day method and stage table. Without your planting date it assumes April 25. It reproduces North Dakota's table exactly, but it hasn't been compared with stages observed in fields, the table was developed in North Dakota, and it doesn't yet cover grain fill, maturity or winter wheat.[^agweather-spring-wheat-stages]
- The [wheat scab model](/models/wheat-scab) gives the chance of a Fusarium head blight epidemic for wheat flowering on the day shown. It doesn't yet take its flowering date from the stages model, so check your own field's stage before you read it; the [scab page](/topics/wheat-scab) explains its limits.
