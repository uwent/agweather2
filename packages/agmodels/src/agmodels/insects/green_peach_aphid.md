## What it predicts

The green peach aphid is the aphid that most often settles and builds colonies on potato, and it spreads potato virus Y and potato leafroll virus efficiently. The model shows how many winged green peach aphids a typical Wisconsin suction trap catches each week through the season, as a typical catch and as a share of the season's peak. Winged aphids in the traps show when they are flying into fields, which is when new colonies start and when the viruses spread.

It shows typical pressure for the time of year, not a forecast of this year's flight.

## How it works

The curves come from Wisconsin's suction traps, tall traps that catch flying aphids each week, in 2010 and from 2014 to 2023. A smooth curve was fitted to each week's catch of green peach aphids by calendar date, allowing for differences between years and traps; another was fitted by degree days, counted from January 1 by the single-sine method, base 39.2 °F (4 °C) with a cutoff at 86 °F.

The model reads the calendar curve unless you choose degree days, because the calendar fitted the catches better. Green peach aphids fly in late summer as their host plants mature and the days shorten, and many arrive on winds from farther south, so the flight follows the season more than local warmth.

In a typical season:

- A few winged green peach aphids are caught from May.
- The flight builds from about August 8 (10 % of the peak), passes half the peak about August 21 and peaks about September 1.
- It falls to half the peak about September 13 and stays low into the fall.

By degree days, the flight reaches half its peak at about 2,900 and peaks at about 3,410.

## How to read it

- Risk levels compare the week's typical catch with the season's peak: Very high means the flight is usually near its peak at this time of year.
- In central Wisconsin's scouting counts, green peach aphids were rarely found on leaves before mid-August. Scout leaves for small colonies of wingless aphids and act on what you find in your field.
- For seed potatoes, the flight builds through August and peaks about September 1. Keep aphid and virus protection in place through late August, and consider killing vines before the late-summer flight peaks.

## Validation and caveats

- Draft (model version 0.1, beta). Fitted with each year or each trap left out, the calendar curve described the held-out catches better than degree days at any base temperature tried. Neither could time a single trap's season, which can be weeks off the typical one.
- The traps are all in Wisconsin. Elsewhere the model runs with a warning: the calendar curve describes a Wisconsin season.
- The traps show winged aphids in flight, not the aphids on your plants. Field counts of green peach aphids were too few to fit a curve of their own.
