# Map image

A finished map of a layer as a PNG image, ready to place in a web page, newsletter or report. It has county and state boundaries, a frame with lines of latitude and longitude labelled at its edges, a title naming what is mapped (such as "Precipitation, cumulative total" or "Growing degree days, single sine 50–86 °F, cumulative total"), the dates below it (an hour in Central time, with UTC beside it) with how many days or hours a combined map covers, such as "Jun 1 – Aug 31, 2026 (92 days)", a legend, and a caption giving the data sources and, where any days or hours were estimated or missing, the most at any location.

Without a date, the map shows the latest day, so a link such as `/api/v2/maps/dd_modified_50_86.png` always shows the current growing degree day map. With `stat` and no start date, the map combines the days from January 1, so `/api/v2/maps/degree_days.png?dd=sine_50_86&stat=sum` is this season's degree days by single sine 50–86 °F. For degree days, `biofix` starts the total on another day (`biofix=04-15`). `days=7&stat=sum` shows the past week's total, for example of rain. The layer's settings are the same parameters as for `/grids/{layer}`.

The colors span the map's own range of values unless you set `scale`, so two maps can use the same colors for different values; give `scale=default`, or the same range for both, to compare maps color for color. `isolines` adds labelled lines of equal value. When you save the image, your browser offers a file name that includes the layer and the dates and settings you chose, such as `soil_temperature_4cm_mean_min_2025-07-01_2026-07-01.png`.

`roll` shows a rolling window instead of one day: `/api/v2/maps/air_temperature_max.png?date=2026-06-19&roll=14` is the 14-day average of the daily high ending June 19, and `precipitation_sum.png?roll=7` the past week's rain. Add `roll_align=centered` to center the window on the date.
