# Chances of a freeze by date

The chance of a freeze for each day of the year at one or more locations, from the past 30 years unless you choose a shorter period. A freeze is a day whose minimum temperature reached the threshold: 32, 28 and 24 °F unless you choose others.

Each row is one location, threshold and day, with a `season` and a `chance` in percent:

- In spring (January through June), `chance` is the share of the years in which a freeze came after that date. In spring it falls toward 0 as the season goes on: a chance of 10% on May 10 means that one year in ten had a freeze after May 10.
- In fall (July through December), `chance` is the share of the years in which the first freeze had come on or before that date. In fall it rises toward 100%.

Chances are given among the years with enough data, which `/climate/frost` counts. They describe the past years' weather on our grid, and are guidance, not a forecast. Chances are available only inside the area we cover.
